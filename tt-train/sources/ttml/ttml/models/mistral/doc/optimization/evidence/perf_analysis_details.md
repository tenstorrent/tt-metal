# Performance analysis: details

Companion to `../../perf_analysis.md`. Same section names; the prose that does not fit a bullet lives here, lightly condensed, numbers unchanged. Paths are relative to `doc/` unless they start with `O/` (= `doc/optimization/evidence/`).

## Setup and clock

Setup for every number unless stated: chip 0 of the QB2 box (110 cores, 31.83 GiB), seq 1024, batch 1, LoRA r16/a32 on all four block linears, AdamW, bf16, no recompute. MFU numerator: 96.37 TFLOP per 40-layer step (`doc/plan.md` §3; `useful_flops_per_step`), i.e. 2.3406 T per layer + 2.749 T fixed; single-chip denominator 148.5 TFLOP/s (110 cores x 1.35 TFLOP/s, the `ttml.common.performance` convention, i.e. the peak at the nominal 1350 MHz); TP=4 denominator 4 x 148.5 = 594 TFLOP/s. The full model does not fit one chip (`doc/plan.md` §4), so single-chip MFU runs are at 8 of 40 layers and the 40-layer single-chip figures are projections from the depth fits, labelled as such. Plan target: 1.62 s/step, 40.0 % MFU (±10 %) at the nominal clock, stretch 1.26 s / 51.6 % with a memory-bound LoRA path.

**Clock, and how to read the Tracy numbers.** The chip does not run at 1350 MHz under this load. From the raw cycle counters in the Tracy CSVs (`composite/evidence/clock_analysis.txt`, `sources/examples/mistral_train/clock_analysis.py`): step period in device cycles over the host step period gives 1187 / 1171 / 1146 / 1130 MHz at 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 layers, and step cycles over the non-profiled wall-clock 1194 / 1185 / 1172 / 1160 MHz; the idle-dominated step 1 gives 1349.2-1349.9 MHz at every depth (the control: the counter runs at AICLK, and the chip is at 1350 MHz only when idle). `tt-smi -s` during an 8-layer run: `composite/evidence/clock_trace_8layers_tt-smi.txt` (summarised in the composite README). Tracy converts cycles to ns at the clock it read at device open (`CHIP_FREQ[MHz]: 1350` in every `profile_log_device.csv`), so every Tracy "ms" in these documents is a cycle count divided by 1.35 GHz: it is the kernel's cost in cycles, and the per-cycle efficiency ("% of peak") it implies is the fraction of the *delivered* peak. Wall time = Tracy ms x 1350 / f_delivered. With f = 1.16 GHz (8 layers) that factor is 1.164.

**Full-model convention (plan.md scope change).** The 40-layer MFU and loss runs are TP=4 (weights column/row-sharded over 4 chips, vocab-parallel embedding and LM head, collectives inside every linear), reported against 4 x 148.5 TFLOP/s; the single-chip projection stays as the kernel-work yardstick (no collectives, one chip's clock). The TP=4 full model at 598.7 ms is 4 x 598.7 = 2395 chip-ms per step against 1933-1985 projected single-chip wall-ms: the difference (~20 %) is the collectives (all-reduce/all-gather per linear, replicated vocab-parallel logic) plus whatever the per-chip clock does in the mesh; it is attributed in the loop's first TP profile.

**Priority change (iteration 4, 2026-10-29).** The user ranks the full-model TP=4 MFU run as the result of every iteration; single-chip Tracy at 1/2/4/8 layers is kept only to rank the next items, and no single-chip MFU run or 16-layer profile is taken from iteration 4 on. HiFi4 with fp32 accumulation stays mandatory (HiFi2 rejected).

**Validation against the non-profiled wall-clock.** Naively, wall - Tracy device = 14.0 / 22.0 / 34.7 / 63.6 ms at 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 layers (14-15 % of the wall, fit 7.2 + 7.03 L ms); the first draft of this document called that the host/dispatch gap. It is not: it is the clock conversion. In cycles (`clock_analysis.txt`, steady step 3): at 8 layers the step spans 501.75 M cycles, the kernels sum to 500.87 M (device busy 99.8 % of the span), and the next step's first kernel starts 1.65 ms later; the host enqueues the whole step in ~45 ms and then waits. At 1 / 2 / 4 layers the in-step idle is 0.5 / 1.1 / 0.2 % and the inter-step idle 1.5 / 1.6 / 2.2 ms. So wall = Tracy ms x 1350 / f_delivered + ~2-4 ms, with f from a measurement independent of that wall: f = 1130 MHz (the profiled run's cycles over its own host period) gives 370.4 x 1350/1130 + 2.5 = 445.1 ms against the warm-chip 443.8 ms (+0.3 %) and the cool-chip 434.0 (+2.5 %); the tt-smi in-run mean of 1151 MHz gives 437.1 ms (-1.5 % / +0.7 %). The delivered clock itself moves 1130-1160 MHz with the thermal state, which is the 434 vs 444-453 ms spread of the MFU runs. Projected 40 layers: 1658.6 ms of cycle-time, 1933 ms (1160 MHz) to 1985 ms (1130 MHz) wall, 33.6-32.7 % MFU, with the wall fit's 1947 inside. The host/dispatch class is ~2-4 ms per step, < 1 %, far below the plan's 58 ms estimate, and is not a loop target.

## Iteration 5 (2026-10-29): batched TP all-reduce of the partial LoRA gradients

**Attribution (TP=4 4-layer profile, iteration 4, chip 0; `O/iter4_tp_ranked_chip0.txt`).** Besides the per-layer tensor-parallel all-reduces (17 x reduce-scatter `[1024, 5120]` at 268 us + all-gather at 135 us per 4 layers, ~54 ms per step: inherent), the LoRA paths issued 16 reduce-scatters of a `[1024, 32]` tensor per 4 layers (plus 16 all-gathers of `[256, 32]`): the `h` all-reduce of a row-parallel layer and the `dh` all-reduce of a column-parallel layer (`fused_lora.py`, `lora.py`), each a 64 KB collective that costs 130-214 us of latency, 3.4 ms per 4 layers, ~34 ms per step on the waiting chips.

**Change (`fused_lora.py`, `lora.py`).** The reductions are linear, so they can be deferred: a row-parallel layer adds `s * h_r B^T` with the rank's *partial* `h_r` to its partial output before the one output all-reduce (`sum_r (x_r W_r + s h_r B^T) = x W + s h B^T`), so `dh = s g B` is full on every rank and `dB_r = s g^T h_r` is a partial; a column-parallel layer keeps `dA_r = s dh_r^T x` as a partial. `fuse_lora_into_frozen_weights(model, batched_tp_grads=True)` marks the parallel wrappers (`tp_partial_grads`), the forward skips the collectives, and `reduce_partial_lora_grads(model, tp_axis)` (called by `train.py` after the backward) concatenates the partial `dA`s on their row dim and the partial `dB`s on their column dim and all-reduces the two stacks once per step (the two collectives cost 28 + 26 us; the stacking itself is not free, see iteration 5b's measurement). This iteration covers the fused wrappers (gate_up column-parallel, w2 row-parallel at TP-local shapes); qkv and out run on the composite adapter at TP (their local shapes `[5120, 1536]` / `[1024, 5120]` are not in the transposed allow-list) and keep their two per-layer collectives (next).

## Iteration 9 (2026-10-30): residual add fused into an all-core RMSNorm

**Attribution.** From the Loop-resumed ranking: `RMSNormForward` on 32 cores (one core per tile-row, 153 us per `[1024, 5120]` call, 1.37 ms per 4 layers per chip) and the `[1024, 5120]` adds (59 us each): per layer two residual adds in the forward and, in the backward, two autograd accumulations of the two gradients reaching each residual stream (through the norm and through the residual path), 16 of the ~30 plain adds per 4-layer step. **Change.** New metal op `metal/ops/add_rmsnorm_fw_allcore/` (`ttml.ops.metal.add_rmsnorm_fw_allcore(residual, delta, gamma, eps) -> [sum, out, rms]`): phase A adds the two inputs (fp32 DEST), writes the bf16 sum (the next residual stream) and a per-(tile-row, slice) partial sum of squares; phase B folds the partials into `rms = sqrt(rowsum / C + eps)` (fp32 `1/C`, not the bf16 scaler of `rmsnorm_fw`), writes the rms tile and `sum * gamma / rms`. Same `choose_work_split` as the backward (3 slices of 54 tiles, 96 cores). The all-core backward takes an optional `residual_grad` added to `dL/dx` inside its apply phase (one more streamed input, no separate add). **A latent circular-buffer hazard, fixed in both all-core norm kernels.** A tt-metal CB producer writes contiguous L1 and `cb_push_back` only wraps on an exact hit of the buffer end ("no other wrap is legal", debug-only assert). The ported `rmsnorm_bw_allcore` streams `block = 4` tiles per push into 8-tile CBs over 54-tile slices (13 pushes of 4 and one of 2), so a core's *second* work item starts its first push 2 tiles short of the end and overruns the CB. With 96 items on 110 cores no core has a second item, which is why iteration 6's tests and profiles never saw it; a `[1, 2, 1024, 5120]` input (192 items) does. The new op found it first (`skinny_matmul`, iteration 10: PCC 0.01 at K=5120, a ragged last slice). Fix in all three kernel sets: every streamed CB moves in full `block` pushes and pops, the shorter tail of a slice reads fewer tiles into a full block, and the compute kernels pad their per-tile outputs to a full block so the writers' pops never straddle either. Harness cases with 64 tile-rows (two items per core) were added for the backward and the fused forward.

## Iteration 11

Why the gain is smaller than the core-balance arithmetic. Before: forward and Q backward scheduled 128 (row, St-1-row) pairs on 110 cores, so 18 cores did two pairs (66 K/V tiles of work) while the rest did one (33): a 1.7x headroom if the kernels were compute-bound per row. The KV backward scheduled 64 K/V rows on 64 cores, the longest row 32 Q tiles x 4 heads = 128 units against an even share of 38: 3.3x. Measured: forward 268 -> 229 us, Q backward 271 -> 216, KV backward 460 -> 289 (+ 28 of reduce). Each kernel processes one Q tile-row (forward, Q backward) or one (K/V row, head) (KV backward) per item and re-reads the other operand from DRAM for every item: forward and Q backward read K and V up to the diagonal, 8 heads x 528 tiles x 8 tiles x 2 KB = 67 MB per call (280-300 GB/s at the new times); the KV backward reads Q, dO (4 + 4 bf16 tiles) and the lse and u tiles (fp32) per (row, head, Q tile): ~100 MB per call (~350 GB/s). The box's DRAM delivers ~400 GB/s to one op in practice (the all-core norm kernels run at ~420), so the three kernels are now within 15-30 % of the bandwidth bound of their single-row access pattern, and further balance cannot help. The next rung is Q-chunking: several Q tile-rows per item (a chunk of 2-4 rows against each K/V chunk) so K/V are read once per chunk instead of once per row, which divides the traffic by the chunk size; it needs the online-softmax state (max, sum, running output) per row of the chunk in L1 and a DEST budget of 4 fp32 tiles per acquire, i.e. a restructure of the inner loops of all three kernels (`SDPA_OPTIMIZATION_PROPOSALS.md` lists the related items).

The LPT weights: forward `round_up(row + 1, Sk_chunk_t) + 1`, Q backward `row + 2`, KV backward `(St - row) * heads_per_item + 1`; the `+ 1` stands for the per-item fixed cost (Q read, normalisation, output write). With 256 items on 110 cores the heaviest core carries 39-40 units against an even share of 38.4.

Standalone wall times (one chip, `O/iter11_baseline.log` vs `O/iter11_sdpa_check.log`): q8 kv2 S1024 fw 282 -> 238 us, bw 759 -> 568; q32 kv8 S1024 fw 901 -> 882, bw 2335 -> 2178; q8 kv8 S1024 (MHA, no head split, work list only) fw 279 -> 238, bw 680 -> 552; q4 kv1 S1024 fw 173 -> 144, bw 564 -> 346; q8 kv2 S2048 fw 887 -> 827, bw 2212 -> 1984; q6 kv2 S512 D64 fw 46 -> 47, bw 195 -> 126.

## Iteration 23 (2026-11-01): SDPA chain hop overlap (kernel boost)
