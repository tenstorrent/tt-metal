# What tt-blaze does differently, and what is reusable for DSV4.1-Flash decode (fast dispatch, trace, <=120 cores)

Date 2026-10-03. Agent h46b. Read-only study of `/mnt/tt-data/ssinghal/inworld/tt-blaze` (nothing built or run there) plus one device microbenchmark on host .47.
Overlay: `/mnt/tt-data/ssinghal/wt/h46b` (diff: `/mnt/tt-data/ssinghal/wt/h46b/changes.diff`), log `/mnt/tt-data/ssinghal/dsv4-logs/h46b_dram_mm_custom.log`.
Tags: **[V]** verified (read in code, or measured by me), **[I]** inferred/estimate.
Candidates are ranked by KERNEL-TIME change, not launch count (our gaps are ~4% of a layer; see 2026-10-03-dsv41-megakernel-feasibility.md).

## 0. Summary

1. **Blaze is a different machine design, not a faster implementation of ours.** One layer per 8-chip (4x2) stage, a pipeline of stages joined by sockets, ONE token (M=1) in flight per stage, weights resident (L1 SRAM / DRAM) per stage, one never-returning kernel per stage under slow dispatch [V: docs/model_writers_contract.md:44-50, blaze_101.md s4]. Its only DSV4 number is `dsv4_hca_layer` Pro ~164.5 us/iteration (8 chips, 1 token, 1000-iteration loop) [V: tests/blaze/perf_baselines/deepseek_v4.json]. Ours is 40 layers on 32 chips at batch 16: ~1.2 ms/layer. The per-layer numbers are not comparable (blaze spends 8 chips x 1 layer; we spend 1/40 of a Galaxy per layer and amortise weight streaming over 4 tokens/device).
2. **The blaze Flash port is incomplete**: attention placements are `()` ("Flash attention is not exercised yet"), routed-MoE/DRAM grids are "placeholder", hidden 4096 / 256 experts (ours: 5120 / 384) [V: deepseek_v4_flash_blaze_config.py]. Attention/layer harnesses only run `dsv4_pro`. No DSV4 op has a multi-token / batch>1 path.
3. **Measured on our silicon (new): the expert matmul is DRAM-bound at bfp8, not compute-bound, and 32-row padding does not cost time there.** One-chip microbench on the 8 DRAM-bank worker cores, K/N = our expert shapes (5120x2304 gate/up, 2304x5120 down), M=4 real rows [V]:

| shape | weights | b1 `DRAMStreamingMatmul` (custom_mm, M=4 tiny tiles) | standard ttnn DRAM-sharded matmul, M padded to 32 | bytes/GB/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gate/up 5120x2304 | bfp8 | 37.6-38.0 us (PCC 0.9996) | 35.5 us | 12.5 MB, 330-353 GB/s |
| down 2304x5120 | bfp8 | 37.4-37.6 us | 36.6 us | 342 GB/s |
| gate/up | bfp4 | 26.2-26.3 us (PCC 0.9931) | 31.6 us | 6.6 MB, 210-253 GB/s |
| down | bfp4 | 26.2 us | 33.9 us | 196-254 GB/s |

   (traced, 50 replays, includes ~3-5 us fixed op cost. fp32 dest vs bf16 dest: +0.3 us. PCC vs the unquantised fp32 torch matmul, so it includes the weight-quantisation error.)
   Consequences: (a) moe_compute streams an expert (37.6 MB bfp8) in 83 us = 453 GB/s: that is the DRAM roofline, so "more cores per expert" or "no 32-row padding" cannot speed up the bfp8 expert; (b) at bfp4 the padded 32-row matmul stops following the byte reduction (bfp4/bfp8 time ratio 0.89-0.93 padded vs 0.70 with M=4 custom_mm), so tiny tiles become necessary the moment weights get smaller (bfp4 or mixed precision), (c) `custom_mm` already exists on our main tree and works through our `generic_op`/b1 path, no fork needed.
   The moe_compute "compute-bound" statement in the task brief is therefore not supported at bfp8 [V]; it is bandwidth-bound plus load imbalance.

## 1. How blaze fuses a layer [V unless tagged]

* **Graph -> program.** `with blaze.fuse()` builds a graph of micro-ops; `BlazeCompiler.compile` makes a `FusedProgram`; `.run()` = `ttnn.generic_op(io_tensors, build())` (program.py:1076). A FusedOp is a tree of `emit()` calls (Mcast -> Matmul -> ResidualAdd -> Gather ...). Fusion order = depth-first emit order; ops on disjoint cores run concurrently, ordering within a core is emit order only.
* **Engines.** CBEngine assigns CB ids (<=64, `MAX_CB_ID`), SemEngine assigns one `GlobalSemaphore` per gather/mcast/CCL sync point, role flags (`f.flag(name, cores)`) become per-core named compile-time args, CTArgEngine resolves everything into `ct_args::<prefix>::` structs in a generated header.
* **Per-RISC codegen** (`kernel_codegen.py`): one generated .cpp compiled for NCRISC/BRISC/TRISC; `unified_kernel_descriptor.py` groups cores with identical CT args and builds one kernel set per group; `if constexpr (role)` removes dead phases per core. `kernel_main` = init() all phases; `while(true){ phases in emit order }`; teardown. Adjacent runtime-branch (`If/Else` on `BlazeMetadata`) phases collapse into one branch.
* **Intermediates.** Between ops: CBs (FIFO, sharded-L1-backed), "streamless" L1 scratch (`f.l1_scratch()`, DM-only, TRISC cannot touch it), or registers/DST. CB ids beyond 64 are reused across phases by rewriting the per-core CB table in L1 (`cb_reconfig*`, 264 words/core), with core-aware graph colouring so CBs on disjoint cores share an id. L1 arena lifetimes: spatial (disjoint cores) and temporal reuse, opt-in.
* **Dataflow patterns.** Activation is mcast from a sender core to all compute cores (one persistent NOC command-buffer profile per program, `mcast_state.py`), weights are core-local (L1) or streamed from the bank-adjacent DRAM worker, partials are gathered to one core, residual add + next mcast. Weights never move through the NoC for matmuls.
* **Weights.** (i) L1-resident width-sharded bfp4/bfp8 (the "blazing" part, "shoving weights into L1"), attention capped ~800 KiB/core; (ii) DRAM-streamed by `dram_streaming_matmul`: one worker core per DRAM bank pinned adjacent via `get_pinned_optimal_dram_bank_to_logical_worker_assignment(NOC_0)`, K-contiguous column sticks, triple-buffered CB, `custom_mm` LoFi compute; (iii) `compressed_tensor`: per-tile bfp8/bfp4 chosen by a 2-bit map under a bits budget (default 3.5 b/elem) read by `compressed_custom_mm`; (iv) hot/cold experts: most-routed experts SRAM-resident (bfp4), the rest DRAM, from a routing-frequency JSON.
* **Persistence / per-token flow.** Persistent mode loops until a host-written termination global semaphore; tokens arrive through sockets; per-token branching by `BlazeMetadata` mcast. Compile+dispatch of a 4x2 stage can take ~5 min, done once. Non-persistent single-shot is the default (`LoopingConfig(run_persistently=False, num_iterations=1)`; 512 `.run()` sites in tests) [V: kernel_codegen.py:41-84].
* **DSV4 mapping.** mHC = `dsv4_mhc_{pre,mid,post,sinkhorn,sigmoid,weighted_sum,mid_comb_res}` (each on ONE core; root device only; Sinkhorn packs up to 64 4x4 matrices per tile on one core; fn weights bfp4 in L1). `attn_res*` is Kimi-K3 block AttnRes, NOT mHC. Attention: `hca_q_branch` (wq_a, rmsnorm, mcast, per-head wq_b on 1 core/head, per-head norm, partial RoPE), `hca_kv_window`, `compressor_{update_state,core,end,kv_cache_update}`, `hca_flash_mqa` (fork of FlashMLA decode, 8 S-blocks x 8 cores, attention sink via phantom peer), `csa_attention` (+indexer: `indexer_q_branch`, `distributed_indexer`, `indexer_local_topk` with `topk_xl`), `hca_post_sdpa` (grouped o-proj: wo_a per group on 64 cores, wo_b on 112 cores, reduce_to_one). MoE: `deepseekv4_flash_moe` = CclBroadcast+RMSNorm+Mcast, `deepseekv4_moe_router` (gate matmul with fused sqrt(softplus), gather, `GenericMoeGate`), clamped SwiGLU DRAM-streaming experts on 8 bank cores, `shared_expert` (64+64+64 cores), `reduce_to_one`.
* **Core partition (Pro HCA harness, 130 cores)**: qa 48, wq_b 64, Q-head 16, kv 16, compressor 16 + cache 16, wo_a 64, wo_b 112, flash 64, mHC 3 single cells, sender (12,9), phantom gate column 12 [V: tests/blaze/fused_ops/dsv4_mhc_attn_block/harness.py:455-532]. Groups overlap in time but are asserted disjoint where concurrent.
* **Measured by blaze**: only per-stage us baselines (`tests/blaze/perf_baselines/*.json`) and TSU gates for other models; no DSV4 Flash end-to-end.

## 2. Our tree vs the "blaze-metal fork"

* The README's "fork" is stale: the pinned tt-metal commit `d2f4b3afeca` is an ancestor of our HEAD (our tree is 1254 commits ahead); no blaze-only delta at the pin [V, sub-agent git ancestry check]. Named CT/RT args (`get_named_compile_time_arg_val`, `blaze_named_*` on `KernelDescriptor`, `tt_metal/hw/inc/experimental/blaze_named_args.h`) are in our tree but marked experimental / "will be deleted (#50953)" [V]. C++20 JIT is NOT on main and NOT needed (no C++20 constructs found in blaze kernels) [V].
* LLK/compute APIs present on our main (`tt_metal/hw/inc/api/compute/experimental/`): `custom_mm.h` (ours is newer: any kt_dim 1..256 vs even-only), `compressed_custom_mm.h`, `custom_mm_reuse_dest_srcb.h`, `sdpa*.h`, `topk_xl.h`, `softmax_k.h`, `hadamard.h`, `rmsnorm.h`, `rope_sfpu.h`, `pack_rows_to_addr.h`, `add_rsqrt.h`, `sinkhorn.h`, `generic_moe_gate.h`/`generalized_moe_gate.h`, semaphore compute API [V]. Missing under the blaze names: `deepseek_moe_gate.h` (ours is generalized_moe_gate), a few `llk_math_*moe_gate_transpose*` and binop headers; vendored blaze `kernel_includes` (81 files) mostly differ textually from ours, some behaviourally (topk_xl, sdpa, rope, top32) [V sub-agent]. Treat blaze op headers as pattern sources; port against OUR experimental headers.
* Host APIs blaze needs (generic_op, MeshProgramDescriptor, SemaphoreDescriptor, global semaphores, sockets, fabric Python) exist [V]. Fabric edm headers changed since the pin: re-test any fabric-using op.
* `models/demos/deepseek_v3_b1` in our repo is the open-source ancestor: it has `dram_streaming_matmul` (M=1/4/8 tests), `dram_streaming_experts_matmul`, `dram_streaming_matmul_compressed`, `matmul_custom_compressed`, `compressed_tensor`, flash_mla, rope, rmsnorm, gather/mcast, moe gate [V]. Blaze adds on top: the Python compiler/engines, DSV4-specific ops (mHC, CSA/HCA, compressor, indexer, clamped swiglu), cb reconfig, hot/cold, sockets, many model families. For our purposes b1 + our overlay already contains the kernel-level pieces that matter.

## 3. 130 vs 120 cores, fast dispatch, persistence

* **Verified on silicon here** (fast dispatch, our test fixture): `compute_with_storage_grid_size() = 12 x 10` = 120 worker cores, DRAM grid 8x1, DRAM-bank worker cores (NOC_0) at (0,9),(0,0),(0,7),(0,3),(7,9),(7,1),(7,6),(7,4) [V: log line 1-2]. Blaze's default is 13x10 = 130 with DRAM workers at the same 8 positions [V: grid_config.py:115]. So the loss is exactly column x=12.
* Blaze's 112-core "matmul grid" = 130 - 8 DRAM workers - the last column (10) [V: grid_config.py `build_matmul_core_grid`], i.e. columns 0-11 minus the 8 DRAM workers = 112 on a 120-core device as well: **the matmul grid survives unchanged**. What breaks: everything that lives in column 12: sender core (12,9), the gate-matmul "phantom" cores (12,0..9), (11,8)/(11,9) gate cores for Pro, `build_ab_grids()` (128 cores split 64/64: now ~116, so 58/58), 12-core gate grids, and `deepseek_v4_blaze_config.py` hard-codes (shared down `get_matmul_cores()[:56]`, LM head 101 cores (0..9,0..9)+(10,0) is fine). `GridConfig.from_device` reads the device grid so re-tiling is mostly mechanical; per-op counts that assume 64/48/112 need re-picking. Our own kernels use 32-56 cores, so 120 is not a limit for us.
* **Persistent kernel in fast dispatch**: not possible for a never-returning program (it would hold the CQ and the dispatch cores' worker slots; blaze itself requires `TT_METAL_SLOW_DISPATCH_MODE=1`, asserts it in `model_pipeline.py:211`, uses `enable_asynchronous_slow_dispatch`). **But blaze does not need persistence to be used**: the default single-iteration build is a normal `ttnn.generic_op` program. One program per sub-block per token inside our trace is exactly our existing pattern (tt/mhc_kernels etc.) and is what any port would do. Persistent loop, sockets, stage-sync, `BlazeMetadata` are NOT reusable and not needed.

## 4. Ideas judged by kernel time (per layer, T=4/device, our numbers from the op table)

Legend: saving is kernel us on the critical path, [I] unless measured. "Reuse" = what to copy.

| # | idea | what blaze/b1 does | expected kernel saving | effort | reuse / verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Byte reduction for all DRAM-streamed weights: per-tile mixed bfp8/bfp4 (`compressed_tensor`, budget ~3.5-5.5 b/elem) with M=4 tiny-tile `compressed_custom_mm`** | `blaze/compressed_tensor`, b1 `dram_streaming_matmul_compressed`, our `compressed_custom_mm.h` | experts: 83 us -> ~55-60 us per expert at ~30% fewer bytes (x3-5 experts = 80-140 us/layer, i.e. 6-10%); shared expert 103 -> ~70 (-30); needs accuracy budget (all-bfp4 chained PCC 0.969 vs 0.990 known) | HIGH for moe_compute (monolithic ttnn op, would need its own compressed kernel or replacing it with b1 `dram_streaming_experts_matmul` + our dispatch/combine), MEDIUM for shared expert/attn linears | **#1 lever and the only one that moves the DRAM roofline.** Prerequisite: M=4 tiles (see row 2), measured below. |
| 2 | **M=4 tiny tiles (custom_mm) instead of 32-row padded tiles** | b1 `unified_kernels/dram_streaming_matmul.hpp`, `custom_mm.h` | 0 us at bfp8 (measured DRAM-bound 335-350 GB/s either way); **-5 to -8 us per matmul at bfp4** (26.2 vs 31.6/33.9) | LOW to try in shared expert / q/kv linears (b1 op is directly usable through generic_op, as in my test) | Make it the default compute tile for any bfp4/mixed weights; does nothing alone. |
| 3 | Attention front end as ONE multi-core kernel: wq_a/wkv matmul -> split-core RMSNorm (q_lora 1280 and kv 512 norms, today 13.6 + 7.2 us on 1 core) -> wq_b -> per-head norm -> RoPE -> head layout | blaze `hca_q_branch`, `hca_kv_window`, `create_q_heads` (M=1, 16-core wq_b, rope_sfpu.h), our `attn_pre.py/attn_kernels` already partly do this | single-core norms 21 us -> ~4; `nlp_create_qkv_heads_decode` (17 us, 4 cores) -> folded into epilogue; 2x rope chains (multiply+linear+addcmul ~16 us each, x2) -> in-kernel: **~40-60 us/layer** | HIGH (M=1 -> M=4 redesign; blaze ops assume M=1, 16 heads/dev) | Copy structure only; kernels must be rewritten for T=4 rows and 5120 hidden. |
| 4 | Activations stay in L1 across ops (no DRAM round trip, no 1.5-3.8 us tiny-op kernels) | CB/L1 scratch chaining | per tiny op 2-4 us: 45 ops < 5 us in L2 (all slice/typecast/to_memory_config/reshape/eltwise): realistic **20-40 us/layer** from fusing them into their producers (this is "kernel time" of ops whose real work is ~0) | MEDIUM per site; includes router chain and compressor chain | Router chain already largely done by router_select (25-40 us); compressor chain ~25 us -> ~12 on 4 layers. |
| 5 | Remove single-core stages | blaze has none better: its mHC pre/mid/post/Sinkhorn and gate topk are ALSO single-core cells | 0 new; ours: mhc post (46 us 1 core in op table, 24 us total mixes after h47p), router topk 63 us -> replaced by fused gate | n/a | Nothing to copy; blaze's `dsv4_mhc_sinkhorn` packs 64 4x4 per tile on 1 core, ours is equivalent. |
| 6 | Overlap all-reduce / all-gather with compute inside the kernel | blaze `all_reduce` is exactly 2 devices on the axis, `all_gather` ring of 4, `reduce_to_one` mesh shapes {1x2,4x1,4x2,4x4}: none fits a 4x8 mesh [V] | n/a: attention CCL 44 us + MoE allgather 25 us stay ttnn ops | VERY HIGH | **Not reusable.** |
| 7 | Whole-layer megakernel | blaze does it with 8 chips/layer, M=1, ~770 KB-1.4 MB L1 to itself | not realistic: L1 budget (~770 KB static CB region because moe_compute keeps ~650 KB/bank), M=4, fp32 mHC, 4x8 CCL, non-persistent only. Merging mHC mixes saved ~5 us (h47p); launch gaps are 4% | n/a | Don't. |
| 8 | Hot/cold experts (SRAM-resident hot experts) | blaze `hot_cold_setup.py` | per hot expert hit: 83 us -> ~0 DRAM time, but L1 per expert 20 MB (bfp4) = 170 MB/chip aggregate L1 total; routing at batch 16 is nearly uniform (3.8 distinct experts/device) | HIGH | Not viable at 384 experts / 40 layers. |

* **Expert imbalance is bigger than any of this** (moe_compute median 282-358 us vs max 463-522 us; reduce_scatter reads 181-194 us in trace as straggler wait [V op table]). Blaze avoids it by design (M=1, 6 experts, one stage). Not a fusion problem.
* Honest bottom line: nothing in blaze lets us beat the DRAM roofline except lowering bytes (row 1). The kernel-time wins that blaze-style fusion can deliver are rows 3-4: ~60-100 us/layer (4-7%), at high effort.
