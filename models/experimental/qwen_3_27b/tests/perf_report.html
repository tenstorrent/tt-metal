Headline

The bottleneck flips with sequence length. At T=1 the input projections are the single largest cost at 51.5% and the recurrent scan is only 33.4%. By T=32 the scan is 93.2%, and at T=128 it is 96.3%. Decode and prefill need different fixes.

The projections are already running near DRAM-bandwidth roofline, so decode has little headroom without batching. The scan is entirely ours to fix — it is dispatch-latency bound, not compute bound.

Finding 1 — a layout tax that only exists for T > 1

At T=32 the three layout ops are 343 of 865 dispatches (40%) and 5.7 ms of 31.8 ms (18%) — doing no arithmetic. Slicing token t out of a TILE-layout [B,H,T,128] tensor is not tile-aligned, so every slice becomes untilize → slice → tilize. At T=1 the tensor is a single row and none of it is needed.

Matmul cost per call is flat — 189.8 µs at T=1, 189.6 µs at T=32 — for a [1,48,1,128] @ [1,48,128,128] product. Identical work per call at every T means these are pure dispatch latency.

Finding 2 — projections are at roofline, except two of them

The four _project matmuls at T=1, with achieved weight-read bandwidth:

qkv and z sustain ≈210 GB/s, a large fraction of this part's DRAM bandwidth. They are weight-bandwidth bound and cannot get meaningfully faster at batch 1 — the only lever is batching, which amortizes the weight read across users.

b and a are the outlier. 48 output columns pad to 64, which is two tiles, so each matmul lands on 2 of 64 cores and runs 16× slower per byte. Together they burn 104 µs — 8% of project, 4% of the entire T=1 layer — to produce 96 numbers. Fusing them into one [5120, 96] projection, or folding them into in_proj_qkv, recovers most of that. Small in isolation; it repeats across 48 layers.

What this means

  • Decode is capped at ≈8 tok/s by this layer alone. 2.58 ms × 48 GDN layers ≈ 124 ms/token, before attention layers, MLPs, or the LM head.
  • Roughly half of decode cost is irreducible at batch 1. The projections are at bandwidth roofline; only batching helps.
  • The chunked scan is the prefill fix, and it wins twice. It replaces 3T small matmuls with a handful of [64,128]-shaped ones — attacking both the dispatch-latency ceiling and the per-token layout conversions, which disappear when tokens are processed in tile-aligned blocks.
  • Fusing in_proj_b and in_proj_a is a cheap, isolated win worth ~4% of decode time.

Reproducing this

  1. Confirm the build has the profiler

    grep ENABLE_TRACY build_Release/CMakeCache.txt   # ENABLE_TRACY:BOOL=ON

  2. Run the workload under Tracy

    python3 -m tracy -p -r -o /tmp/prof \
    -a device_kernel_duration --op-support-count 40000 \
    -m 'pytest models/experimental/qwen_3_27b/tests/test_perf.py -k deltanet'

    -r is required or no ops CSV is produced. --op-support-count defaults to 1000; exceeding it fails post-processing with a missing-op assertion rather than a clear error.

  3. Summarize

    python models/experimental/qwen_3_27b/tests/summarize_perf.py /tmp/prof

    Stage attribution comes from signpost pairs wrapped around the stage methods by the test, so the module itself carries no profiling code.

Read these numbers with four caveats

  • Wormhole n150, not Blackhole. The target is 8×P150; absolute figures will move, the shape of the breakdown should not.
  • Random weights. This measures arithmetic and dispatch, not model quality.
  • Recurrent scan, not chunked. The chunked path is not yet implemented — that is what these numbers argue for.
  • Single device, batch 1, prefill path. No tensor parallelism, no trace capture, no multi-user batching.
