# Optimized decoder — Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct on Blackhole

Stage 02: same decoder layer as stage 01, made faster. Semantics, paged KV
cache, non-aligned sequence lengths, determinism and the 262144-token context
contract are all preserved.

|---|---|---|---|
| prefill S=128 | 540.85 µs/tok | 73.26 | **7.38×** |
| prefill S=512 | 536.54 | 69.12 | **7.76×** |
| prefill S=1024 | 535.87 | 68.90 | **7.78×** |
| prefill S=2048 | 536.21 | 69.38 | **7.73×** |
| traced decode ctx128 | 1.5655 ms | 0.5634 | **2.78×** |
| traced decode ctx1k | 1.6618 | 0.6617 | **2.51×** |
| traced decode ctx4k | 1.9942 | 0.9916 | **2.01×** |

Every cell above is copied verbatim from the `us_per_token` / `median_ms`
columns of `perf_prefill.csv` and `perf_decode.csv` in this directory and in
`../functional_decoder/`; the ratios are those cells divided.

**These CSVs are rewritten by every run of the perf tests**, which the plain
`pytest .../tests/` in *Verification* includes. Re-measurement moves the third
significant figure — the functional S=128 cell has moved between 540.6 and
542.3 µs/token across runs of this code, the optimized one between 71.64 and
73.26, and ctx128 between 0.5615 and 0.5634 ms — so this table is the state of
the CSVs on disk and a rerun will not reproduce the last digit. Run the
correctness suite with `-m "not models_performance_bare_metal"` if you want the
published numbers left alone.

The "before" column is the *functional* layer as it stands in this tree, and it
is regenerated by the same command — stage 02 deliberately keeps its router
rewrite in `optimized_decoder.py` rather than in the shared
`functional_decoder.router_forward`, so that the baseline these ratios divide
into stays the stage-01 measurement rather than drifting with stage-02 work.
(It was tried the other way round first: putting the router rewrite of
`work_log.md` §7 into the shared module moved the baseline itself to
538.44 µs/token at S=128 and 1.5400 ms at ctx128.)

Layer PCC against HF clears the 0.995 bar at every length (0.99870–0.99910,
`test_optimized_prefill_vs_reference` over S = 32, 128, 512, 33, 100, 257).
Expert weights are bfloat4_b and attention projections bfloat8_b, so ~0.0005 PCC
is deliberately spent — measured on real weights, not assumed.

## What changed

**`in0_block_w`: 1 → 16/12 (3.0×).** Stage 01 inherited `in0_block_w=1` from the
exemplar's config helper. With K=2048 (64 tiles) that feeds the expert matmul
one tile of the inner dimension per pass, which is what pinned it at ~5.4% of
peak FLOPs — not core count, not precision, not expert sparsity. The two matmuls
have different K, so the widths are per-role: 16 for gate/up, 12 for down.

**bfloat4_b expert weights (2.2×).** Invisible until after the block-width fix —
at `in0_block_w=1` the kernel is latency-bound, so weight dtype cannot matter.
The two knobs interact; see the joint sweep in `work_log.md`.

**DRAM-sharded decode attention projections (1.11×).** Once the experts were
fast, `o_proj` and `qkv` were 21% of decode device time, which made the stage-01
"attention is 0.08% of prefill, no action" call stale.
`MatmulMultiCoreReuseMultiCastDRAMShardedProgramConfig`, with the weight
width-sharded one shard per DRAM bank and the activation and output width-sharded
in L1, takes qkv 68.3 → 46.8 µs and wo 96.0 → 41.7 µs measured standalone and
eager (those carry per-call dispatch; in the archived profiles the same two ops
go 57.06 → 27.33 µs and 72.80 → 21.91 µs).

On the whole traced layer the number depends on what the "before" leg holds, and
this stage published two incompatible pairs. The like-for-like comparison —
everything else at the shipped configuration, both legs on the same bfloat8_b
weights, only the program config and shard layout differing — was re-measured at
the end of the stage and is **0.6508 → 0.5863 ms at ctx128, 1.11×** (traced,
median of 100, the same harness as `perf_decode.csv`). Both legs predate the
router change below, so the sharded leg is 0.5863 rather than today's 0.5634;
it landed on the `perf_decode.csv` ctx128 cell of that day, 0.5864, which is
what corroborates it. The 1.19× that appeared elsewhere came from a `0.697 ms`
baseline that no measurement in this stage reproduces; it is retired, not
merely rounded.

**Packed gate+up (1.09×).** `_sparse_matmul_config` parallelises only over N, so
N tiles cap usable cores: 768 → 24, 1536 → 48, 2048 → 64. Packing doubles
gate/up's cores. A caution worth carrying forward: measured at the stage-01 block
width, packing looks like a **1.66×** win; against a properly tuned separate
candidate it is **1.09×** — most of the apparent gain belonged to the block-width
fix. Re-checked at the end of the stage on the whole traced layer: separate
0.735 ms vs packed 0.673 ms. Tune both sides before attributing a win.

**LoFi on the expert matmuls (1.05×).** bfloat4_b carries 4 mantissa bits, so
HiFi4's extra passes have nothing to resolve. Prefill 72.46 → 69.13 µs/token
(the §4 sweep in `work_log.md`, at the adopted block width 16), and layer PCC is
0.99910 at LoFi against 0.99909 at HiFi4.

**bfloat8_b attention projections (1.02×).** Decode 0.6726 → 0.6605 ms at PCC
0.99906 vs 0.99909 (the §6 sweep in `work_log.md`, measured before the
DRAM-sharded change above), and it nearly halves the attention term of the
roofline.

**Deleting the router's two keepdim reductions (1.045×).** The router and its
routing prep are 111.6 µs of decode device time — 20.9% of the 534.8 µs
post-A–G profile, more than either matmul family — and went four reviews
without a finding of their own. (Over the stage-01 region the same 111.6 µs is
7.4% of 1513.9 µs, where `SparseMatmul` alone is 69%.)
Most of the removable part is not arithmetic: `ttnn.max` and `ttnn.sum` each
drag a `FillPad` behind them when the last two dims are not tile-aligned, which
on an 8-wide top-k tensor costs 10.42 and 10.41 µs against 1.43 and 1.41 µs of
reduction. `router_forward_optimized` takes the max from column 0 of the sorted
top-k and moves the sum after the scatter, where the reduction length is 128 —
a whole number of tiles, so no padding lane can enter it. Traced ctx128,
0.5866 → 0.5615 ms (four legs back to back in one process), with routing
*identical*: `test_optimized_router_matches_functional` asserts the same 8
experts in the same slots at S = 1, 33 and 128.

Dividing after the scatter divides over whole tiles, and the tile row-padding
has a zero numerator *and* a zero denominator, so unguarded `ttnn.div` returns
**+inf** there where the functional router returned exact zeros. Nothing
observable leaks — every consumer was traced, and decode at S=1 was clean
anyway — but the divisor is clamped regardless, which costs **+1.6 µs on
decode, 0.28%**, and is why `perf_decode.csv` now reads 0.5634 rather than the
0.5615 of the unguarded A/B. `test_optimized_router_padding_is_zero` pins the
invariant at S = 1, 33, 100 and 128. See `work_log.md` §7 for the padding
table, the rejected free version, and for what else did not work — including a
`ttnn.softmax` over the 8 survivors that is faster still and silently wrong.

## What was tried and rejected

Every row below was built and measured, not rejected on a first API error.

| candidate | result |
|---|---|
| Per-token prefill sparsity | **2.1× slower** (14.35 vs 6.70 ms). Forces M=1, which pads back to a 32-row tile: a 32× waste in M swamps a 16× saving in experts. The dense prefill in gemma4/gpt_oss is forced by the op contract. |
| `output_tile=Tile([1,32])` on the decode sparse matmuls | **1.07× faster and unusable.** Removes the 32× M padding at the source, but no downstream op reads a 1×32 tile correctly — `slice` rejects it, `sum`/`reshape` raise `MeshBuffer must be large enough`, `untilize` returns wrong data silently, `fast_reduce_nc` returns zeros. |
| Keeping the expert path rank-6 (dropping all three reshapes) | **0.713 vs 0.673 ms** — 6% slower. The reshapes are not overhead: they compact the M padding so the elementwise ops touch 192 tiles instead of 6144. |
| Folding the routing scale in before `down` | **tie** (0.5852 vs 0.5862 ms, one run, at the pre-§7 configuration). The variant that used `fast_reduce_nc` on the padded tensor was 0.6316 ms, 8% worse. |
| `ttnn.softmax` over the 8 top-k survivors | **33.7 µs faster and wrong** (98.3 vs 131.9 µs on the router-only trace). It reduces over the whole 32-wide tile, so padding lanes enter the denominator and the 8 weights sum to 0.9736 instead of 1. Rejected on numerics; `work_log.md` §7. |
| Sum-of-8 as a matmul *before* the scatter | 0.5650 vs 0.5615 ms — slower than summing after it, and it would read the top-k tensor's padding lanes. |
| Sum-of-8 as a ROW_MAJOR `ttnn.sum` (no `FillPad` on row-major input) | 114.79 vs 109.75 µs on the isolated router trace. Slower than the matmul. |
| Scatter kept in ROW_MAJOR to skip the layout round trip | 0.5619 vs 0.5650 ms against the same leg — a real 3.1 µs, but it only *moves* the conversion (see `work_log.md` §7) and is incompatible with the adopted sum. Not taken. |
| bfloat4_b attention projections | **PCC 0.9928**, below the 0.995 bar. Rejected on accuracy, not speed. |
| Explicit fidelity/accumulator configs on the attention matmuls | All ≤ the ttnn default. LoFi/HiFi2/HiFi4 are within noise of each other; what matters is `packer_l1_acc`, which the default already enables. |
| tt-perf-report: in0 in L1 | sparse rows 1.001×, attention rows 0.998×. Noise. |
| tt-perf-report: `out_subblock_w` 2 / 4 | 1.001× / 1.001×. Noise. |
| tt-perf-report: HiFi2 on the sparse rows | 69.78 µs/token, between LoFi (69.13) and HiFi4 (72.46). Superseded by LoFi. |

## Performance accounting

| | ms/token/layer |
|---|---|
| roofline estimate (42.074 MB @ 512 GB/s) | 0.0822 |
| device time (sum of the 59 decode kernel durations) | 0.5127 |
| end-to-end (blocking traced replay, ctx128) | 0.5634 |

The 42.074 MB counts the block-float formats at their true footprint, not at a
round byte per element: a 32×32 tile is 576 B at bfloat4_b and 1088 B at
bfloat8_b, i.e. 0.5625 and 1.0625 B/element, because each 16-element block
carries its own exponent byte. An earlier revision of `perf_summary.json`
dropped that overhead and reported 38.53 MB / 0.0753 ms / 14.1%.

**16.0% of roofline** against device time, 14.6% end to end. The dominant
remaining gap is that `sparse_matmul` pads M to a full 32-row tile, so decode at
M=1 does 32× the necessary row work: the three `ReshapeView` ops that compact it
away are 111.12 µs, **21.7%** of decode device time — larger than either matmul
family. (The `ReshapeViewDeviceOperation` line in the op-code table is 22.3%;
the extra 0.6 points is a fourth, 3.39 µs reshape in the layer tail, which is
not part of the compaction. Both numbers are in `topology_audit.md`; the three
compaction reshapes are the ones this paragraph is about.) That is a TTNN
op-contract limit; both alternatives were measured and are in the table above.
See `perf_summary.json` for the full derivation.

**Those four numbers are `tt-perf-report` row IDs, not CSV line numbers.** The
same four ops are rows **47, 65, 90 and 98** (0-based) of
`ops_perf_optimized_decode.csv`; the report's ID column runs two ahead. Every
citation in this directory now says which of the two it means.

Report rows 49 and 67 are the decode attention projections: `BFP8` weights,
`LoFi`, 74.9% and 74.8% of peak bandwidth, 27.33 and 21.91 µs. The like-for-like
interleaved rows are 50 and 67 of
`../functional_decoder/tt_perf_report_decode_paged32.txt` — the same two
projections in the same decode region of that profile — at **57.06 µs, DRAM,
73.4%** for qkv and **72.80 µs, SLOW, 46.1%** for `o_proj`. (The prefill-region
duplicates of the same two ops, rows 3 and 13, read 73.7% and 46.0%.)

So the win is **not** "DRAM rather than SLOW" across the board, and this
directory said that for two revisions. Only `o_proj` was SLOW-classified; qkv
was already DRAM-classified at 73.4%, and its gain is the 2.08× drop in
duration, which comes from the weight being bfloat8_b *and* read one shard per
bank rather than from a change of class. Report rows 92 and 100 (CSV rows 90 and
98) are the experts: `BFP4`, `LoFi`, 48 and 64 cores, matching the
packed-vs-separate core-occupancy argument above.

**Three different core counts describe these same two rows, and all three are
true of different things.** Stated once, here:

| number | what it counts | where to check |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | banks the *weight* is sharded over, one shard each — the tuned quantity, swept against 16/32/64 in `work_log.md` §5 | `../../tt/optimized_decoder.py`, `_bank_row(_DRAM_BANKS)` |
| **80** | the profiler's `CORE COUNT` column for the two projections | `ops_perf_optimized_decode.csv`, rows **47 and 65** (0-based) |
| **12** | what `tt-perf-report`'s core column prints for the same two ops | the quoted block above, report rows **49 and 67** |

Only the 8 is a decision. The other two are how the profiler and the report
describe the launch, and neither was tuned.

## Not done in this stage

1. Batch coverage stops at **32**, and that is now a TTNN op limit rather than a
   harness one. `test_optimized_decode_batch` runs B = 1, 2, 8 and 32 in
   process, each user against its own HF reference plus the broadcast-spread
   guard. B=64 fails in
   `nlp_create_qkv_heads_decode_device_operation.cpp:51`,
   `num_users <= num_users_supported` with `num_users_supported = 32`
   hardcoded at line 45 — and that op is on both the DRAM-sharded and the
   interleaved attention path, so the cap is not something this layer chose.
   See *Batch coverage* in `work_log.md` for the two self-inflicted limits that
   had to be removed first.
2. Decode activations are width-sharded in L1 across the two attention
   projections but remain DRAM interleaved elsewhere; measured, that made no
   difference (see the rejected table).
