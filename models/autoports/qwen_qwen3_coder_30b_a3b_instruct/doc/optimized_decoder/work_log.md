## What actually mattered, in order

### 1. `in0_block_w`: 1 → 16/12  (3.0× on gate/up, 2.3× on down)

Stage 01 inherited `in0_block_w=1` from the exemplar's config helper. With
K=2048 (64 tiles) that feeds the kernel one tile of the inner dimension per
pass. This one constant — not core count, not precision, not expert sparsity —
is what held the expert matmuls at ~5.4% of peak FLOPs.

Swept on real weights at M=32 (bf16 weights, the stage-01 dtype):

| in0_block_w | packed gate+up (K=2048) | separate gate (K=2048) | down (K=768) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8.051 ms | 6.735 ms | 3.456 ms |
| 2 | 4.230 | 3.573 | 1.837 |
| **4** | **2.699** | 2.062 | **1.483** |
| 8 | 2.729 | **1.476** | 1.503 |
| 16 | 2.935 | 1.480 | FAIL |

### 2. Packing gate+up: 1.09×  (not the 1.66× it first appeared to be)

`_sparse_matmul_config` parallelises only over N, so N tiles cap usable cores:
768 → 24, 1536 → 48, 2048 → 64. That cap explains stage 01's profile directly —
`down` ran at 127 GB/s while gate/up sat at 64 GB/s on identical data.

**The size of this win was nearly misreported.** Measured at the stage-01 block
width, packing looks like 1.66× (13.376 → 8.069 ms), and that figure was
reported mid-stage in good faith. The `$optimize` checklist requires a packed
candidate to beat a *well-tuned separate* one, so the separate path was re-tuned
— and the advantage collapsed to **1.09×** (2 × 1.476 = 2.952 ms vs 2.699 ms).
Most of the apparent gain was the block-width fix credited to the wrong change.

Re-confirmed at the end of the stage on the whole traced layer rather than on a
matmul microbenchmark: separate gate/up **0.735 ms** vs packed **0.673 ms** at
ctx128. Packing is kept, and the checkpoint already stores gate/up fused as
`[E, 2I, H]`, so this undoes a split stage 01 introduced to mirror the exemplars.

### 3. Precision: bfloat4_b, worth 2.2× — the first answer here was wrong

The stage initially concluded that precision had no effect, from a sweep where
bf16/bfp8/bfp4 × LoFi/HiFi2/HiFi4 all measured ~0.209 ms/token, identical to
three decimals. Stage review challenged that null, and it did not survive.

The sweep was run at `in0_block_w=1` — before the fix in §1. In that regime the
kernel is latency-bound, so the weight dtype cannot matter. The block width was
then tuned **for bf16** and the search stopped. Two knobs, each swept alone,
each hiding the other's benefit.

Joint sweep, real weights, M=32 (prefill's shape), ms:

| blk | packed bf16 | bfp8 | bfp4 | bfp4/LoFi | | blk | down bf16 | bfp8 | bfp4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 2.697 | 2.371 | 2.367 | 2.366 | | 4 | 1.489 | 1.062 | 1.058 |
| 8 | 2.726 | 1.669 | 1.445 | 1.431 | | 6 | 1.507 | 1.002 | 0.785 |
| 16 | 2.932 | 1.806 | **1.259** | 1.149 | | 8 | 1.507 | 0.993 | 0.712 |
| 32 | 2.910 | 1.734 | 1.158 | 1.153 | | 12 | 1.513 | 0.941 | **0.654** |
| 64 | 3.180 | 1.789 | 1.372 | 1.204 | | 24 | 1.622 | 0.982 | 0.733 |

bf16's optimum is 2.697 + 1.489 = 4.186 ms; bfp4's is 1.259 + 0.654 = 1.913 ms.
Block width must divide K in tiles and the two matmuls have different K, so the
widths are per-role: 16 for gate/up (K=2048), 12 for down (K=768).

Real-weight layer PCC for each candidate, all above the 0.995 bar:

| dtype | S=128 | S=512 | S=33 |
|---|---|---|---|
| bf16 | 0.99951 | 0.99941 | 0.99901 |
| bfp8 | 0.99955 | 0.99944 | 0.99905 |
| **bfp4 (selected)** | 0.99909 | 0.99900 | 0.99857 |

The lesson is the generalisable part: a one-knob-at-a-time sweep found a local
optimum and produced a confident, wrong conclusion.

### 3b. The M=1 (decode-shape) block-width sweep

The table above is at M=32. Decode runs the same two matmuls at M=1, where the
op pads M back to a full tile, so the optimum could in principle differ. It was
measured separately and does not — but the data was collected and never
published, so it is recorded here.

**Decode shape, M=1**, bfp4 weights, median ms, grid 8×6 for gate/up and 8×8
for down:

| in0_block_w | gate_up (K=2048, grid 8×6) | down (K=768, grid 8×8) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.191 | 0.113 |
| 8 | 0.133 | 0.090 |
| 12 | illegal — 12 does not divide 64 | **0.086** |
| 16 | **0.114** | — |
| 24 | illegal | 0.088 |
| 32 | 0.114 | — |
| 64 | 0.124 | — |

The legality rule is that the block width must divide K in *tiles*: 2048/32 = 64
for gate/up, so {1,2,4,8,16,32,64}; 768/32 = 24 for down, so {1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24}.
12 is legal for down and illegal for gate/up; 16 and 32 are the reverse. Both
matmuls are flat across their minimum (16 and 32 tie at 0.114 on gate/up; 8, 12
and 24 are within 5% on down), so the M=32 choice of 16/12 is also at the M=1
optimum and one pair of constants serves both phases.

### 5. DRAM-sharded decode attention projections, 1.11×

After §1–4 the expert path was no longer the whole story. Re-reading the decode
profile:

| | share of decode device time |
|---|---|
| `SparseMatmulDeviceOperation` (experts) | 18.5% |
| `MatmulDeviceOperation` (qkv + o_proj + router) | 24.7% |
| `ReshapeViewDeviceOperation` (expert-path compaction) | 18.4% |

Those three shares are from an **intermediate** profile taken after §1–4, which
is not archived — the two profiles kept in `doc/` are the stage-01 functional
one and the final optimized one. Its absolute per-op durations (qkv 55.6 µs,
o_proj 73.1 µs, router 24.5 µs) are therefore not citable, and nothing else in
this stage should quote them; the archived interleaved costs of the same two
projections are **57.06 µs** (qkv) and **72.80 µs** (o_proj), rows 48 and 65 of
`../functional_decoder/ops_perf_decode_paged32.csv`, which agree with the
intermediate profile within run-to-run noise.

Either way the stage-01 audit's finding G — "attention is 0.08% of prefill, no
action" — was stale for decode. tt-perf-report also flagged the qkv row directly: *"Try a
DRAM-sharded program config (MatmulMultiCoreReuseMultiCastDRAMShardedProgramConfig)
to improve throughput further."*

At decode M=1 both projections are pure weight reads, so what limits them is how
well the read spreads across the DRAM banks. Sharding the weight one shard per
bank, with the activation and output width-sharded in L1 over the matching core
row, measured at the op level (bfloat8_b weights, eager, median of 50):

| matmul | interleaved | 8 cores | 16 | 32 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qkv (K=2048, N=5120) | 68.3 µs | **46.8** (1.46×) | 50.3 | 59.1 | n/a |
| wo (K=4096, N=2048) | 96.0 µs | **41.7** (2.30×) | 45.8 | 55.2 | 84.5 |

These are standalone eager calls with a host sync around each one, so they carry
dispatch that the same ops do not pay inside a captured trace; they are for
ranking the core counts against each other, not for quoting as the op's cost.
The archived in-layer durations are 57.06 → 27.33 µs (qkv) and 72.80 → 21.91 µs
(wo) — `../functional_decoder/ops_perf_decode_paged32.csv` rows 48 and 65
against `ops_perf_optimized_decode.csv` rows **47 and 65** (CSV line numbers,
0-based; the same two ops are `tt-perf-report` rows 49 and 67).

8 cores — exactly one per DRAM bank — wins on both. Past that the extra cores
only add mcast traffic.

**On the whole traced layer this stage published two incompatible pairs, and
only one of them is like-for-like.** They were reconciled by re-measuring at the
end of the stage, with both legs at the shipped configuration (bfp4/LoFi
experts, block widths 16/12, packed gate+up, bfloat8_b attention weights) and
the *only* difference being whether the sharded weight copies exist — dropping
them sends `attention_decode_optimized` down its interleaved fallback, which
reads the same bfloat8_b weights. Traced ctx128, median of 100, same harness as
`perf_decode.csv`:

| decode attention projections | traced layer, ctx128 |
|---|---|
| interleaved | 0.6508 ms |
| DRAM-sharded (shipped) | **0.5863 ms** |
| | **1.11×** |

Both legs predate §7, so the sharded leg reads 0.5863 rather than today's
0.5634. At the time it landed on `perf_decode.csv`'s ctx128 row of 0.5864 — the
same configuration the perf test measured — so the sharded leg was corroborated

The `0.697 → 0.587 ms` (1.19×) that this file, the README and the module
docstring all carried is **withdrawn**: no measurement in this stage reproduces

**Core counts: 8, 80 and 12 all describe these rows.** The tuned quantity is
**8** — banks, one weight shard each, the column swept in the table above. The
profiler's `CORE COUNT` for the two optimized rows reads **80**, and
`tt-perf-report` prints **12** for them. Only the 8 was chosen; see the table in

Prefill cannot use it. A plain `ttnn.linear` on a DRAM width-sharded weight

`../context_contract.json` has carried 20.05/40.11 throughout. Attention is 0.8% of
prefill, so there is nothing to recover on that side.

### 6. Attention projection dtype: bfloat8_b, 1.02×

Swept against the **ttnn default** compute config. The first pass of this sweep
was invalid and is worth recording as a trap: passing any explicit
`compute_kernel_config` also turns off `fp32_dest_acc_en` and `packer_l1_acc`,
which the default enables, and that alone cost ~0.001 PCC — so "HiFi2" looked
worse than "default" when the difference was really the accumulator.

| wqkv/wo dtype | prefill S=512 µs/tok | decode ctx128 ms | layer PCC |
|---|---|---|---|
| bf16 | 72.55 | 0.6726 | 0.99909 |
| **bfloat8_b (selected)** | 72.48 | **0.6605** | 0.99906 |
| bfloat4_b | 72.50 | 0.6595 | **0.9928 — FAIL** |

bfloat4_b is 0.2% faster than bfloat8_b and fails the 0.995 bar, so it is

### 7. The router block: deleting both keepdim reductions, 1.045×

Findings A–G were all expert-path or attention items. Nothing in this stage ever
put a number on the router *and its routing prep* as a block — the audit's only
router sentence was about its matmul, 24.57 µs in the stage-01 profile and
24.46 µs in the optimized one — and the block is large:

| | µs | share of decode device time |
|---|---|---|
| router + routing prep, stage 01 — `../functional_decoder/ops_perf_decode_paged32.csv` rows 68–88, still on disk | **111.6** | 7.4% of that 1513.9 µs region |
| the same block after A–G — pass-4 read 111.7 µs off the then-current optimized profile, which this section's re-profile has replaced | **111.7** | **20.9%** of 534.8 µs |
| after this section, before the divisor guard | **87.8** | 17.2% of 511.7 µs |
| after the guard — `ops_perf_optimized_decode.csv` rows 69–88, the profile on disk | **88.9** | 17.3% of 512.7 µs |

A–G never touched the block — it is the same 21 ops doing the same work, which
is why the stage-01 profile and the post-A–G one agree to 0.1 µs — so its
*share* tripled while nothing about it moved. That is the same shelf-life
problem finding G had, seen from the other side: A–G shrank everything around it.

**Where the 111.6 µs went**, off the stage-01 profile (identical code, and the
only archived profile that still contains the pre-fix block):

| op | µs |
|---|---|
| `TopK` | 26.27 |
| router `Matmul` | 24.57 |
| `FillPad` ×3 (one for `topk`, one for each keepdim reduction) | 4.20 + 10.42 + 10.41 |
| hand-written softmax proper (`Reduce`/`BinaryNg`/`Unary` ×6) | 15.81 |
| `Untilize` ×4 + `Tilize` (the tile↔row-major round trip around `scatter`) | 6.84 + 5.61 |
| `Scatter` | 3.17 |
| `Slice` ×2, `Typecast` ×2 | 1.75 + 2.57 |

Two thirds of the *removable* cost is not arithmetic. `ttnn.max` and `ttnn.sum`
call `fill_implicit_tile_padding` whenever either of the last two dims is
unaligned (`ttnn/cpp/ttnn/operations/data_movement/fill_pad/fill_pad.cpp:17-24`),
and the top-k tensor is 8 wide and, at decode, 1 row tall. So each keepdim
reduction dragged 10.4 µs of padding fill behind 1.4 µs of reduction.

**Adopted.** `router_forward_optimized`:

* the **max** is column 0 of the top-k output — `sorted=True` guarantees it —
  so a 0.87 µs `slice` replaces `FillPad + Reduce`;
* the **sum** moves *after* the scatter and becomes a matmul against a column of
  ones. Over the dense row the reduction length is 128, a whole number of tiles,
  so no padding lane can enter it.

Whole traced layer, ctx128, real weights, median of 100, one process, all four
legs back to back:

| variant | ms | prefill S=512 µs/tok | prefill S=128 layer PCC |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped before | 0.5866 | 69.14 | 0.9990050 |
| slice-max + sum-of-8 as a matmul before the scatter | 0.5650 | 69.12 | 0.9990050 |
| the above + `scatter` kept in ROW_MAJOR | 0.5619 | 69.17 | 0.9990050 |
| **slice-max + sum after the scatter (adopted)** | **0.5615** | 69.10 | 0.9990057 |

All four legs above predate the divisor guard adopted below; they are an A/B of
the reduction spelling and nothing else, run back to back in one process so they
can be compared with each other. `perf_decode.csv`'s ctx128 cell now reads
**0.5634**, which is the adopted 0.5615 configuration *plus* that guard — the
+1.6 µs is measured separately in the guard's own table below.

PCC is unchanged to six places and the routing is *identical*, not merely close:
`test_optimized_router_matches_functional` compares against the untouched
`functional_decoder.router_forward` at S = 1, 33 and 128 and asserts the same 8
experts in the same slots, with weights close. Two numbers, not one: the
**measured** worst-case gap over S = 1, 33 and 128 is 9.77e-4 — one bf16 quantum
at this magnitude, which is the difference between summing 8 values and summing
the same 8 after a bf16 round trip through the scatter — while the **asserted
bound** in `test_optimized_decoder.py:207` is the looser `delta < 5e-3`, chosen
so the test does not fail on a re-roll of the random hidden state. The test
logs the measured delta on every run, so the 9.77e-4 is re-derivable from the
log rather than pinned by the assert.

**What moving the sum cost, and the guard that pays for it.** Dividing after
the scatter divides over whole tiles, and rows `S`..`ceil(S/32)*32` of the tile
are padding: `dense` is 0 there and, because the sum is over that same padded
tensor, `total` is 0 too. Unguarded, `ttnn.div` returns **+inf** in those rows.
Measured directly with `to_torch_with_padded_shape` (`ttnn.to_torch` slices to
the logical shape and shows nothing):

| S | padded rows | `dense` pad | `total` pad | `div` pad, unguarded | guarded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (decode) | 1..32 | 0 | 0 | **0** | 0 |
| 33 | 33..64 | 0 | 0 | **+inf**, all 31×128 | 0 |
| 100 | 100..128 | 0 | 0 | **+inf**, all 28×128 | 0 |
| 512 | none | — | — | — | — |

It is +inf, not NaN, and decode — the one shape that matters most — was already
clean; only ragged prefill lengths hit it. The functional router, which divided
*before* the scatter, returned exact zeros in the padding, so this is a hazard
the previous path did not have. Every consumer was traced and none observes it:
the sparsity path drops the padding in `to_layout(ROW_MAJOR)`, and the scale
multiply, `rms_norm` and `fast_reduce_nc` reduce along axes that are either
tile-aligned or not the padded one. That is consistent with layer PCC being
unchanged. But "no consumer observes it today" is not an invariant, so:

**Adopted:** clamp the divisor, `ttnn.maximum(total, 1e-30)`. Every real row's
denominator is ≥ 1 — `sorted=True` makes column 0 of `exp_logits` exactly
exp(0) = 1 — so the clamp cannot touch one, and it is measured **bit-identical**
on the real rows at S = 1, 33 and 100. In the padding it turns 0/0 into
0/1e-30 = 0.

**It is not free.** One extra 1-tile binary op, two legs each, one process:

| | decode ctx128 | prefill S=512 |
|---|---|---|
| unguarded | 0.5616 / 0.5613 ms | 69.12 / 69.03 µs/tok |
| **guarded (adopted)** | **0.5633 / 0.5627 ms** | **69.08 / 69.04 µs/tok** |

**+1.6 µs on decode, 0.28%**; prefill is unchanged within noise. Taken anyway:
0.28% is the right price for turning "no consumer happens to look at the
padding" into an invariant, and `test_optimized_router_padding_is_zero` pins it
at S = 1, 33, 100 and 128 so it cannot be optimized back out.

The free version was tried and rejected: fusing the clamp into the existing
matmul as `ttnn.linear(dense, ones, bias=1e-30)` costs no extra op and does fix
the padding, but `ttnn.linear`-with-bias is a different accumulation path from
plain `ttnn.matmul` and moved the **real** rows by up to 1.46e-3 — inside the
test's 5e-3 bound, but it would have made the "routing identical" claim above
false for the sake of 1.6 µs. Its padding was not uniform either (some padding
rows came back 0 and some 1e-30).

**Rejected, with the number.** The "isolated router trace" below is a
router-only trace capture (the same block, a random router weight, median of 100
blocking replays) used to rank candidates cheaply; it carries a fixed
per-replay overhead, so only differences between its rows are meaningful. Every
candidate that survived it was then measured on the whole layer with real
weights, which is the table above.

| candidate | result |
|---|---|
| `ttnn.softmax` over the 8 survivors | **98.3 vs 131.9 µs** on the isolated router trace — the fastest thing measured, and **wrong**. It reduces over the whole 32-wide tile, so the padding lanes enter the denominator and the 8 weights sum to **0.9736**. This is the same class of bug as the 128-wide softmax the constraint forbids, and it is silent. |
| sum-of-8 as a matmul *before* the scatter | 0.5650 vs 0.5615 ms. Slower, and it would read the top-k tensor's K padding lanes, which no op guarantees are finite — the whole reason `FillPad` exists. |
| sum-of-8 as a ROW_MAJOR `ttnn.sum` (row-major input skips `FillPad` entirely) | 114.8 vs 109.8 µs isolated. The untilize costs more than the fill it avoids. |
| keeping `scatter` in ROW_MAJOR to drop the layout round trip | 0.5619 vs 0.5650 ms — a real 3.1 µs, **not adopted**; see below. |
| `FillPad` in front of `TopK` (4.20 µs) | Inside `ttnn.topk`, same `fill_implicit_tile_padding` call, no parameter to skip it. Nothing to do from here. |
| `TopK` itself (26.4 µs, 1 core) | Left alone. It is a single 128-wide row, so there is nothing to spread over cores, and the constraint pins it to fp32 logits. |

**The tile↔row-major round trip cannot be removed, only moved.** `sparse_matmul`
requires its sparsity tensor to be ROW_MAJOR
(`ttnn/cpp/ttnn/operations/matmul/device/sparse/sparse_matmul_device_operation.cpp:105`,
`TT_FATAL(sparsity.layout() == ttnn::Layout::ROW_MAJOR, ...)`), while every
consumer of the same tensor as a *scale* — the routing multiply, and the
normalisation adopted above — requires TILE. `ttnn.scatter` converts all three
inputs to ROW_MAJOR internally and converts the output back to the input's
layout (`scatter.cpp:165`, `:194`, `:233-234`), so exactly one conversion is
unavoidable and the only choice is which side pays it. Measured both ways:
scattering into a ROW_MAJOR `zeros` removes the internal tilize and the
`to_layout(routing, ROW_MAJOR)` in `moe_decode_optimized`, worth 6.2 µs on the
isolated router trace, but the tilize comes back in the MoE tail (and in prefill,
which needs the tiled routing too), leaving 3.1 µs on the whole layer — and it
is incompatible with the adopted sum, which must run on a tiled dense tensor.
So the shipped path keeps one `to_layout` and the 3.1 µs is left on the table
with its reason.

## Rejected, with evidence

**Per-token sparsity in prefill.** The profile reads `active=128/128` and the
checklist forbids a dense all-expert path, so this looked like the headline fix.
`sparse_matmul` indexes sparsity by *batch* dims, not by M, so per-token
sparsity needs a `[1,T,1,H]` layout. Built and measured rather than rejected on
the first API error:

```
tokens-as-batch [1,T,1,H]   nnz 256 (16× fewer)   14.35 ms
dense           [1,1,T,H]   nnz 128                6.70 ms
```

**2.1× slower.** Making tokens batch-indices forces M=1, which the op pads back
to a 32-row tile: a 32× waste in M swamps a 16× saving in experts. The
dense-over-experts prefill in gemma4/gpt_oss is forced by the op contract, not
an oversight. Decode keeps real per-token sparsity, which is free there because
M is genuinely 1.

**1×32 output tiles on the decode sparse matmuls.** This is the most interesting
rejection of the stage, because it is a real win blocked by op support rather
than by the model. At decode M=1 the gate/up matmul writes 12 MB and `down`
writes 16 MB where 0.4 and 0.5 MB is real; the three `ReshapeView` ops exist
only to compact that padding away and cost 31.38 + 33.36 + 46.37 = 111.12 µs,
**21.7%** of decode device time — more than either matmul family. (The
`ReshapeViewDeviceOperation` op-code line is 22.3%; the difference is a fourth
3.39 µs reshape in the layer tail that is not part of the compaction.)

`output_tile=ttnn.Tile([1, 32])` removes the padding at the source. It requires
in0 to carry a matching tile height (`matmul_utilities.cpp:231`:
`out_tile_shape[0] == in0_tile_h`), which `ttnn.tilize(..., tile=Tile([1,32]))`
provides for rank ≤ 4. The matmul then runs, produces numerically correct
output, and the end-to-end step is **1.07× faster** (0.6289 vs 0.6733 ms).

It is unusable because nothing downstream reads the result correctly:

| consumer | behaviour on a 1×32 tile |
|---|---|
| `ttnn.slice` | hard error, `slice_device_operation.cpp:165` compares against a hardcoded `TILE_HEIGHT` |
| `ttnn.sum`, `ttnn.reshape` | hard error, `mesh_tensor_impl.hpp:33` `MeshBuffer must be large enough` |
| `ttnn.untilize`, `ttnn.to_layout` | **succeed and return wrong data** — the round trip does not preserve values |
| `ttnn.experimental.fast_reduce_nc` | **succeeds and returns all zeros** |
| eltwise (`silu`, `mul`) | correct, but immediately re-pad the output to 32 rows |

Two of those are silent, which is what makes this worth writing down. The
rank-5 case also fails to tilize at all. Blocked on TTNN support for non-32 tile
heights outside matmul.

**Keeping the expert path rank-6.** `sparse_matmul` returns
`[1, 1, B, E, 1, N]`, and the decode path reshapes that to a compact `[B, E, N]`
and back. Read off the profile those reshapes look like pure overhead. They are
not — they strip the M padding, so the slice/silu/mul that follow touch 192
tiles instead of 6144. Dropping all three and staying rank-6 measured
**0.713 ms vs 0.673** at ctx128, 0.810 vs 0.770 at ctx1k, 1.142 vs 1.102 at
ctx4k: consistently ~6% slower.

**Folding the routing scale in before `down`.** `down` is linear, so scaling its
input is equivalent to scaling its output, and the input is the compact tensor.
Measured at ctx128 with everything else final:

```
shipped tail (reshape -> mul(routing) -> sum)          0.5862 ms
folded, compact sum                                     0.5852 ms
folded, fast_reduce_nc straight off the padded tensor    0.6316 ms
```

These three legs are one run, taken before §7 — which is why the shipped-tail
leg is 0.5862 rather than today's 0.5634. A second run of the same three read
0.5870 / 0.5853 / 0.6326 and was quoted in parallel with this one in the README
and the module docstring; this triple is the one whose shipped leg matches the
`perf_decode.csv` ctx128 cell of that day (0.5864), and it is now the only one
quoted anywhere.

The first two are a tie; the third is 8% worse, because `fast_reduce_nc`
promotes `down`'s tile padding into the logical shape, so recovering `[1,1,B,H]`
needs a permute plus a slice that cost more than the ops they replaced. The
shipped tail stays.

There is a bug worth recording inside that last row. Before the permute was
added, the tail did `ttnn.reshape(states, (1,1,B,H))` from `[B,1,1,H]`, which
measured 0.550 ms — the best number of the whole stage. It was also silently
wrong for every user except the first, because each batch entry occupies its own
32-row tile and the reshape does not move them. Batch-1 PCC was perfect;
`test_optimized_decode_batch[b2]` caught it at 0.9257. The fix (permute + slice)
is correct and costs more than the shipped tail, which is why the shipped tail
is what ships.

**tt-perf-report's remaining advice**, each measured on the traced layer against
the tuned baseline (0.6519 ms):

| advice | measured |
|---|---|
| place input 0 in L1, sparse rows | 0.6513 ms — 1.001× |
| place input 0 in L1, attention rows | 0.6529 ms — 0.998× |
| `out_subblock_h * out_subblock_w >= 2` (`out_subblock_w=2`) | 0.6513 ms — 1.001× |
| `out_subblock_w=4` | 0.6509 ms — 1.001× |
| HiFi2 on the sparse rows | see §4 — LoFi is better on both speed and PCC |

All four are inside run-to-run noise. Only the DRAM-sharded suggestion (§5) paid.

## batch > 1 decode: root-caused and fixed

Stage 01 supported only a single user, on both the functional and optimized
paths, failing at `sparse_matmul_device_operation.cpp:197` with
`sparsity.logical_volume() == batch_length`. Reading the op source rather than
guessing at layouts explained it:

```cpp
if (is_input_a_sparse && is_input_b_sparse) batch_length = batch_length_B;
else if (is_input_a_sparse)                 batch_length = batch_length_A;
else                                        batch_length = batch_length_A * batch_length_B;
```

`is_input_b_sparse` defaults to **true**. The two expert matmuls therefore land
on different branches:

* gate/up passes neither flag, takes the third branch, and gets `B * E` --
  which is exactly what a `[1, 1, B, E]` sparsity tensor supplies. This half
  already worked once tokens were carried as batch indices (`[1, B, 1, H]`).
* down passes `is_input_a_sparse=True`, so with the default it takes the
  **first** branch and gets `E` alone -- the batch dimension is discarded, and
  no arrangement of A's shape can change that. Five different A layouts were
  tried before reading the source; all returned `batch_length=128`.

Passing `is_input_b_sparse=False` selects the second branch, giving
`batch_length_A = B * E`. Verified standalone at B = 1, 2 and 8.

Carrying tokens as batch indices costs nothing in decode, unlike prefill, since
decode's M is genuinely 1 -- the same layout that made prefill 2.1x slower is
free here.

`test_optimized_decode_batch` covers B = 1, 2, 8 and 32 in-process, giving each
user a distinct prompt so a broadcast bug cannot pass -- and it earned that
design twice, catching the reshape bug described above.

## Batch coverage: two self-inflicted caps, then the real one

Coverage used to stop at 2, and this file recorded that as "a harness reason
rather than a model one". That was true but incomplete: both caps were things
this stage had done to itself, and neither survived being looked at.

**Cap 1 -- the test uploaded 2 GB it never read.** `_build` called
`F.upload_layer_weights` alongside `O.upload_packed_expert_weights`, so every
parametrisation uploaded the full bf16 functional weight set: ~1.2 GB of bf16
experts plus a third copy of wqkv/wo. `decoder_layer_prefill_optimized` and
`decoder_layer_decode_optimized` read exactly three tensors off
`DecoderLayerWeights` -- `input_layernorm`, `post_attention_layernorm` and
`router` -- and take every projection and expert weight from `OptimizedWeights`.
Narrowing `_build` to upload only those three (plus the optimized set) is what
the `bank_manager.cpp:462` DRAM failure was actually reporting. The one test
that needs the full set, `test_optimized_vs_functional_precision_delta`, asks
for it explicitly.

**Cap 2 -- decode's expert intermediates were pinned to L1.** With cap 1 gone,
B=8 moved its failure from DRAM to L1: `Not enough space to allocate 134217728 B
L1 buffer across 110 banks`. That 128 MB is `down`'s output, and it is 97%
padding -- `sparse_matmul` pads decode's M=1 back to a 32-row tile, so the pair
of intermediates costs `batch * 128 experts * 32 rows * (1536 + 2048) cols * 2 B
= batch * 29.4 MB` against ~160 MB of allocatable L1. L1 was a batch-1
affordance being applied unconditionally. `_decode_expert_memory_config` now
places them in L1 only while they fit a 40 MB budget and in DRAM past it, which
is what prefill already does at every length. B=1 is unchanged.

**The real ceiling is 32, and it is a TTNN op limit.** With both caps removed,
B = 8 and 32 pass, each user checked against its own HF reference plus the
broadcast-spread guard. B=64 fails in
`nlp_create_qkv_heads_decode_device_operation.cpp:51`,
`num_users <= num_users_supported`, with `num_users_supported = 32` hardcoded at
line 45 of that file. That op is on the interleaved attention path as well as
the DRAM-sharded one, so nothing this layer chose is what caps it.

Separately, `attention_decode_optimized` would have hit a shard-shape mismatch
rather than a clean failure past B=32: `_width_sharded_l1` hardcodes a 32-row
shard and `_dram_sharded_program_config` sets `per_core_M=1`, both of which are
decode's padded M. `_dram_sharded_usable` now checks the batch as well as the
weight dims and falls back to the interleaved projections. The guard coincides
with the op limit above rather than lifting it -- at B>32 the fallback fails in
`nlp_create_qkv_heads_decode` too -- so it buys a comprehensible failure, not a
working larger batch.

## Harness and bookkeeping fixes

Three defects in the measurement harness were corrupting published numbers.

**`doc/functional_decoder/perf_prefill.csv` was wrong by 8×** — it read
4358–4380 µs/token against a true 536. Cause: the full-suite watcher run
re-executes `test_perf.py`, `TT_METAL_WATCHER=10` inflates device timings ~8×,
and the test truncates and rewrites the CSV. Fixed by marking every perf test
`models_performance_bare_metal` (an already-registered marker) and running
watcher with `-m "not models_performance_bare_metal"`; the file was regenerated
from a clean run. The command is now in the README's Verification block.

**The decode CSVs were opened in append mode**, so reruns stacked. The
functional one had accumulated eight interleaved runs, four of them the inflated
watcher figures, in one file with no run marker. Both decode tests now
accumulate rows in a process-local dict and rewrite the whole file, so a rerun
truncates exactly like the prefill test.

**Prose disagreed with the CSVs.** The README and work log quoted 75.2 µs/token
at S=128 where `perf_prefill.csv` said 77.07. Everything in this directory is
now taken from the CSVs.

`perf_summary.json` also carried two wrong numbers: `attention_weights_mb` was
29.36, which matches no dtype, and `decode_ms_per_token_device` was copied from
the e2e figure with a note saying the two could not be separated. They can: the
ops CSV gives 0.5127 ms of device time (the sum of the last 59 rows of
`ops_perf_optimized_decode.csv`, which is exactly the decode region) against
0.5634 ms of blocking traced replay -- a 51 us dispatch gap.

**The roofline weights were counted at a round byte per element.** bfloat8_b is
1.0625 B/element and bfloat4_b is 0.5625 B/element, not 1 and 0.5: a 32x32 tile
is 1088 B and 576 B respectively, because every 16-element block carries its own
exponent byte. Counting the exponents moves the roofline input from 38.53 MB to
**42.074 MB**, the estimate from 0.0753 to **0.0822 ms**, and the fraction
achieved from 14.1% to **15.4%** of the device time of the day (0.5348 ms; it
reads **16.0%** against §7's 0.5127 ms, which is the figure `perf_summary.json`
now carries). The `named_limitations` are
unchanged -- this makes the model look slightly *better*, and none of the levers
were chosen on it.
