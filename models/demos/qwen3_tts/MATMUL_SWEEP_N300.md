Generated by `tests/test_qwen3_tts_matmul_sweep_n300.py`. Baseline is production today:
`program_config=None` (auto-routing), L1-interleaved activation, LoFi, `fp32_dest_acc_en=True`,
`packer_l1_acc=True`. Timing is Metal-trace replay (16 matmuls/trace x 20 replays, median of 3);
every row's harness baseline tracked the in-model Tracy number within 2-9 %.

**These are isolated single-op measurements and are NOT yet validated in-model.** The
`wshard` rows assume the activation arrives already width-sharded — in the model the producer
must emit that layout or pay an `InterleavedToSharded` (~2-5 us) that is not counted here.

### Findings

* **Every winner is 1D multicast on 32 cores, not 64.** Auto-routing spreads these across all
  64 and loses to a half-grid config on the two big-K shapes.
* **Most of the win is bit-safe.** Keeping `fp32_dest_acc_en=True` still gives -8.7 % / -12.2 %.
  Allowing fp16 accumulate adds little at seq=64 and a further -6 % at seq=128 (it is what makes
  `gate_up` eligible at all).
* **Do not apply one config family blanket-wide.** `o_proj` (K=1024) *regresses* under the same
  width-sharded config that wins elsewhere: +22.9 % at seq=64, +8.6 % at seq=128. Per-shape
  selection is mandatory; two of eight shapes have no win at all.
* **2D block-sharded never places.** M is only 2-4 tiles here, so the mcast grid cannot use the
  M axis — the usual encoder layout does not apply to these short prefill buckets.
* Auto-routing with DRAM-interleaved activations is consistently ~12 % worse than L1.
