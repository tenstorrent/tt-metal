Following one token through the experts

Take token 3,000 of the chunk. It belongs to row 2 (tokens 2,560–3,839), so it starts out split across the four chips of that row, a quarter of its 7,168 values on each.

  1. Attention. Its query is scored against every earlier position it may see: the 46,080 cached tokens plus the chunk tokens before it, about 49,000 keys. Each chip in row 2 does 16 of the 64 heads.
  2. Gather. The row all-gather gives all four chips of row 2 the whole token.
  3. Router. It picks 8 of the 384 experts. Below is an example choice; the real choice depends on the token.
  4. Dispatch. Expert e lives on chip number ⌊e ÷ 24⌋. Each copy of the token only serves experts in its own column, so the token travels straight up or down.
  5. Combine and reduce. Outputs travel back to row 2. Each chip adds up the experts from its column, and the row reduce-scatter adds the four columns together, leaving each chip a quarter of the result.

Numbers come from tt-moe-nappkin's defaults for the Kimi K2.7 preset: layer_costs(isl=5120, kv_cache=46080) on a 4×4 mesh. Attention runs at HiFi2 (213 TFLOPS per chip), routed experts at LoFi (426 TFLOPS), DRAM at 80% of 512 GB/s, and links at 80% of 2 × 200 Gb/s in each direction.

Two defaults matter a lot here. Steps run one after another (with nappkin's "parallel" overlap option, where the shared expert runs during the dispatch and each expert during the previous combine, the expert part drops from 4.46 to 3.76 ms). Expert load is worst case: one expert on each chip receives the whole chunk.

This is the last chunk of the prompt, so attention is at its most expensive. On the first chunk (no earlier tokens) attention takes 1.59 ms and the whole layer 6.16 ms.
