# Router study ledger (RESULTS §1–§98)

One row per change or idea the study built or proposed, in two parts:
- **Part 1, RESULTS §1–§81** (Phase 0; RESULTS.md, V3_INVENTORY.md, variants/README.md), classified against the build
  of its day: router v2 at `TT_FABRIC_EXP_FLAGS=13129088` = s79mc. Its Status column is as of §81.
- **Part 2, RESULTS §82–§98 and the Phase 1 notes** (V3A/V3U/V3L/V3E1_NOTES, V3_REVIEW, HANG_CAPTURE, PHASE1): the
  "Phase 1 part" at the end of this file, classified against the lead.

Numbers are cycles/packet (1.35e9 / packets per s) unless marked ns or GB/s; deltas are against the base named in that
section.

Two columns added 2026-10-26 on every row of both parts, so that the ledger tracks what one ERISC program learned that
the other could use:
- **Applies to**: the ERISC program the idea concerns: E0 (`sender_erisc`), E1 (`receiver_erisc`), both, or test
  (tests, tools, test infrastructure).
- **In the lead?**: whether `fabric_exp/variants/v3q` (the lead since 2026-10-26, RESULTS §98; v3z, v3v6 before it)
  has it: yes on E0, yes on E1, no, or n/a (tools, settings, out of scope). A short note says in what form when it differs
  from the row.

Counts, part 1 (94 classified rows): **IN 31 · LOST-SUBSTANCE 19 · LOST-CONTEXT 8 · LOST-UNCLEAR 3 · MIXED 5 · OPEN 28**.
Measurement/tooling and the DRISC line are listed apart (§7, §8 below). LOST-UNCLEAR = lost, and RESULTS gives no
evidence for substance or for context. Part 2 has its own counts.

Look at these first in a whole-router redesign (the 2026-10-24 list), with where each stands in the current code shape
(2026-10-26). **Re-test** marks the LOST-CONTEXT ideas worth re-testing now, with the reason:
1. **Smaller sender-channel state** (bit 24, s77t, s78l; LOST-CONTEXT ×3). In the lead by construction: E0's slot bounds
   are compile-time, the worker's state is a few scalars and a forwarding channel's a 24 B record (v3l, §86). Not a
   re-test candidate.
2. **Shared runtime forwarding step** (old bit 27, s80; LOST-CONTEXT). Phase 1 tried it twice more: a call per visit
   (v3l, forwarding +19 to +41) and runtime tables (v3u/v3u2/v3u3: VC0 worker rows ~+30, multicast +29, but VC1 −6 to
   −15). **Re-test on VC1 routers only**: it won there (seven channels, mostly idle), and VC1's E0 still grows by one
   inline visit (~440 B) per channel; on VC0 it lost on substance three times.
3. **Loop function boundaries** (noinline main loop §78, separate E0 loop §47, cold connection helpers §79). IN on both
   ERISCs (v3l's E0 loop, v3t's E1 loop, `worker_connection_event`). The rest is IN on E0 since v3z (Phase 1 part A1):
   E0's once-per-round and rare work moved out of its loop function, as on E1 (§97), and an edit there no longer
   re-lays out the pass loop (E0_AUDIT §1, RESULTS §98).
4. **How load groups are expressed** (§46 plain volatile LOST-CONTEXT vs §48 fences IN). Fences are in both programs. Not
   a re-test candidate: the jitter band shows no group fragility (§96, §98). The one-primitive proposal stands
   (V3_REVIEW b#1).
5. **E1 cached downstream credits** (§80). IN since v3e1 (§87).
6. **Worker visit latency** (§73, OPEN). Partly gone, as read on v3v6's E0 (E0_AUDIT §5; not re-read on v3z's): in the
   2D and 1D E0s at -O3/-O2 the semaphore pointer and the worker slot are registers, but on the VC1 E0 (and for the
   pointer at -Os everywhere) the pass still loads them from the stack before the semaphore and payload-size loads.
   These are allocation outcomes; the structural fix is the semaphore address as a compile-time arg (host proposal,
   Phase 1 part E0-16).
7. **E1 completion/deferral shape** (§68, §26, §76). The per-command-buffer check is IN (slot records, v3e1b); the 1D hold
   is IN (v3z, bounded by the ring: Phase 1 part E1-28); ring routers still run the generic loop (OPEN, Phase 1 part
   E1-31).

The other LOST-CONTEXT rows (bit 11, grouped trid flush, idle drain in the `else` arm) were superseded and are not
re-test candidates; `noinline` main loop and cold connection helpers are IN (rows below).

Status key: **IN** in the current build (flag bit in 13129088, or hard-wired in v2/s79mc). **LOST-SUBSTANCE** rejected for a reason independent of codegen (hang, unsafe, inherent work, latency, no effect, user rule). **LOST-CONTEXT** lost, with RESULTS evidence pointing at codegen around it: a re-test candidate in a redesign. **MIXED** a substantive trade-off between rows. **OPEN** proposed, parked or never run.

## 9. User rules (as of §81; current rules: DESIGN_BRIEF.md)

- ERISC-only mode: no DRISC offload.
- No RX work on E0 (the sending ERISC stays lean).
- One worker channel per router; mix flows on a link through forwarded traffic, not a second worker.
- Keep the worker connection check on every pass; never defer it to the outer loop.
- A design that hangs even once is out. Don't probe hang boundaries.
- No register pinning, no allocation-dependent changes, no layout steering (`[[likely]]`, `__builtin_expect`, hot/cold), no compiler-fragile tricks. The source must not assume compiler behavior. README's "Strict E0 Codegen Rules" are observations about GCC 15.1 at -O3, not rules to code by.
- Benchmark-only behavior can't ship, and benchmark-only bits never go in validation yamls.
- Never score telemetry-on runs (`TT_FABRIC_NO_BW_TELEMETRY=1`); the telemetry switch must not ship.
- A design must hold at -O3 and at -Os (VC1 builds).
- Judge whole builds by suite means across configs, opt levels and perturbations. Disassembly explains results; it does not drive edits.
