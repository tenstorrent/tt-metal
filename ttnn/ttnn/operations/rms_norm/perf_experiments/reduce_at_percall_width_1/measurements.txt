6. OPTION 2 -- "can the within-tile column sum ride the square's own DEST window?"
=============================================================================
YES, it is expressible and CORRECT (public compute APIs only: mul_tiles_init(...,
acc_to_dest) / mul_tiles / sfpu_reduce<SUM,.,REDUCE_ROW> / pack_tile, with
eltwise_chain's DestAccumulation::PerRow DEST lifecycle).  It deletes cb_x_squared, its
L1 round-trip, AND the reduce call.  Measured at the focus geometry it is a
MEASURED NON-WIN against the predicate fix:
    fused_per_row  11189 ns (1.03x over baseline) -- 1.84x SLOWER than sq_reduce_tile
    fused_batch     9531 ns (1.21x over baseline) -- 1.57x SLOWER than sq_reduce_tile
MECHANISM: at per-call width 1 all AccumulateViaAdd does is copy_tile + the SFPU
within-tile column fold.  The fused option deletes the copy_tile and the CB round-trip
but KEEPS the SFPU column fold -- i.e. it deletes the cheap half and inherits the
expensive half.  ReduceTile's win comes from replacing that SFPU fold with ONE FPU
matmul-against-ones, which is ~3x cheaper.  Batching 8 tile-rows into one DEST acquire
recovers 1.17x of the DEST-sync cost but cannot close the gap.
An FPU-based fusion (keep the square in DEST, then reduce it with the FPU) is
INEXPRESSIBLE: reduce_tile's operand must arrive through SrcA/SrcB from a CB, and
there is no dest-reuse form of the FPU reduce (EltwiseBinaryReuseDestType exists for
eltwise binary only).  So the L1 round-trip is what pays for the cheap datapath.
