LTX-2.3 bringup · denoiser parallelism

Choosing TP×SP for the LTX-2.3 denoiser on a Blackhole Galaxy

One transformer layer, 32 chips, one degree of freedom: how to split TP · SP = 32. This is the measurement, the model, and the topology reasoning behind the shipped split.

Decision
TP = 4  /  SP = 8

Sits at the floor of the per-layer latency curve for the wall-clock-dominant stage 2 (HD, 38 912 tokens) and within ~9% of optimal for stage 1. Both 1-D extremes lose: a fat SP ring drowns in K/V-gather, a fat TP shard starves the matmul and inflates the feature all-gather.

03 Why the curve is U-shaped

Start from the one fact that removes the red herring:

Per-device compute is invariant to the split. Each chip always does total / 32 — for the matmuls and for attention. Matmul: M·(W/TP) = (N·TP/32)·(W/TP) = N·W/32, the TP cancels. Attention: heads·N²·hd/(TP·SP) = N²·hd, the split cancels. So the choice is not about FLOPs — it is entirely about collective cost and matmul shape efficiency.

What does change are two collectives that pull in opposite directions:

TP feature all-gather bytes ~ M·dim = (N·TP/32)·dim → cost ∝ N·TP (grows as TP ↑)
SP ring K/V gather bytes ~ N·heads_local = N·(32/TP) → cost ∝ N/TP (grows as SP ↑, i.e. TP ↓)

Sum a rising N·TP and a falling N/TP and you get a U with its floor near TP* ≈ √(k_sp/k_tp). Because ring attention is relatively costlier at longer sequence, k_sp/k_tp is larger at stage 2 → the optimum sits at a higher TP there (4) than at stage 1 (2). Add matmul MFU rolloff at high TP — at TP=32 the attn-out projection is only N=128 wide (4 tiles), mcast-bound — and the right wall steepens further.

The two 1-D extremes, concretely

  • TP=1 / SP=32 — no TP collective, but every layer ring-gathers all 32 heads of K/V around a 32-long ring (16-hop diameter). Ring attention balloons: predicted 11.5 ms/layer at stage 2 vs 3.4 ms shipped.
  • TP=32 / SP=1 — architecturally invalid. LTX video self-attention masks SP-padded keys only via ring SDPA's logical_n, which needs SP>1. Plain SDPA at SP=1 would attend padded keys and corrupt the output. It doesn't just lose — it can't run.

04 How the physical torus shapes the answer

A Blackhole Galaxy is 32 chips wired as a native 8×4 2-D torus — wraparound (ring) links on both axes, 4 Ethernet links between adjacent chips at 200 Gbps bi-dir each. A logical 4×8 is the same silicon relabeled. Two properties of that fabric decide where each collective wants to live:

  • A ring axis sustains both directions → ≈2× the bandwidth of a linear (non-wrapped) axis. This is why LTX runs FABRIC_1D_RING and Topology.Ring, not linear.
  • Shorter ring = fewer hops. Diameter is ~N/2: a length-4 axis is 2 hops, length-8 is 4, length-32 is 16. For a fixed tensor the shorter ring wins on latency.

So the placement rule: put the heaviest, most frequent collective on the axis that serves it best. The K/V ring-gather runs in every attention and moves the most bytes → it takes the long (length-8) axis for bandwidth; TP's smaller, matmul-fused all-gather tolerates the short (length-4) axis. That is the precise sense in which matmul-best is one orientation and attention-best is the other — and the shipped sp_axis=1 (len 8), tp_axis=0 (len 4) is the attention-favoring choice, correct because attention is the growing cost at HD.

Open question the sweep answers directly: is SP-on-len-8 (shipped, bandwidth) actually better than SP-on-len-4 (fewer hops)? The *_altaxis rows in the harness transpose the mesh to measure exactly this.

05 The verdict on TP=4 / SP=8

  • Optimal for stage 2, which dominates wall-clock — the measured anchor sits at the modeled floor.
  • Within ~9% of optimal for stage 1; one shared config for both stages avoids a re-shard between them.
  • Balanced 2-D shard — neither collective is allowed to blow up; each op stays near its compute-bound floor.
  • Rides the torus correctly — the dominant K/V ring gets the long, double-bandwidth axis.
