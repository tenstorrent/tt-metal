\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We defend the headline numbers with five exhibits: the 24-cell
competition across five measured arms (Table~\ref{tab:competition}), the
small-k routing before/after (\S\ref{sec:eval-competition}), strong
scaling against the cost model (Fig.~\ref{fig:pscaling}), the chunk-skip
A/B (\S\ref{sec:eval-skip}), and eight production call-site scenarios
(Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}); \S\ref{sec:eval-attribution} closes by
attributing the headline speedup to its parts and pricing the residual
gap to the vendor roofline.

\subsection{Harness and Measurement Methodology}
\label{sec:eval-method}

All measurements in this paper run on a single Tenstorrent \bh{} \pca{}
(\S\ref{sec:bg-tensix}) under exclusive access. Operator-level timings
are device-side kernel durations from the Tracy
profiler~\cite{tracyprofiler}, at operator granularity so host dispatch never contaminates a
number. One device process at a
time, each sweep cell in its own subprocess: caches, allocator state,
and JIT artifacts cannot leak.
Kernel-level cycle constants (\S\ref{sec:characterization}) come from a
math-isolated two-point slope over iteration counts, canceling marker
overhead; each slope run carries a paired control that must reproduce its
known $2.000\times$ ratio or is discarded.

\emph{Correctness gates.} Every performance cell is gated on bit-exact
agreement with a PyTorch golden --- never a correlation threshold --- so
a close-but-wrong kernel cannot post a time. The
competition run of Table~\ref{tab:competition} recorded 0 wrong results
across its 121 gated cells. The surrounding suites hold the same line: a
62-cell numerics contract suite, 154 operator tests, 220/220 routed-path
tests, a 36-check adversarial battery, a $2\times20$-launch hang battery,
and 51/51 plus 21/21 bit-exact benchmark cells (counting; RISC scan).

\emph{Determinism and provenance.} Sweeps are deterministic
(\texttt{torch.randn} bf16 stimulus, fixed
seeds), and every CSV row carries a build fingerprint and source-tree
hash --- each number attributable to one binary and one tree state.

\emph{Trials and spread.} Competition cells report medians of 3 trials;
chunk-skip cells, 3 trials $\times$ 5 measured
iterations with spread below 0.1\% everywhere; the single-core baseline
sweeps carry standard deviations in-file (e.g.,
$9{,}492{,}327 \pm 1{,}391$\,ns). The anchor cell
($k{=}2048$, $N{=}65{,}536$) appears in two independent gated runs ---
34.3\us{} in the competition sweep, 34.4\us{} in the $P$-sweep re-pin;
each is quoted from its own run; the 0.1\us{} disagreement bounds
run-to-run drift. We state the gap plainly: all results come from one
board on one day, and the 121-cell competition run does not commit
per-cell standard deviations --- a cross-day, higher-trial repeat is
scheduled, not done.

\emph{Clock caveat.} Cycle$\to$microsecond conversions in this paper
assume a 1.35\,GHz busy clock; the clock under load was not captured
(the idle clock measures 800\,MHz). Every microsecond figure in this
section is a direct Tracy measurement; the caveat applies only where a
cycle count is restated in time units, flagged where it occurs.

\subsection{Competition Across Five Measured Arms}
\label{sec:eval-competition}

Table~\ref{tab:competition} sweeps $k \in \{512, 1024, 2048\}$ across
$N = 2{,}048$--$262{,}144$ over five measured arms: \emph{pre} --- stock
\ttnntopk before this work; \emph{now} --- stock \ttnntopk today,
including the ${\approx}1.17\times$ replay micro-op win that landed in
the stock kernels; \emph{routed} --- \ttnntopk through the new routing (the caller
changes nothing); \emph{op} --- \topkop called directly (columns
include the SFPLOADMACRO paths, worth 5--9\%); and \emph{proxy} ---
byte-identical stock bitonic kernels in the operator's row-parallel
harness, per-row single-core wall time. Three headlines. First, the user-facing anchor: at $k{=}2048$,
$N{=}65{,}536$, the \emph{pre}$\to$\emph{routed} chain is
$631{,}506\,\text{\textmu s} \to 51.5$\us{} = $12{,}265\times$ --- with no
call-site change. Second, the direct-op anchor: the same cell measures
\textbf{34.3\us{} on 32 cores} ($8{\times}4$ rectangle), an
$18{,}401\times$ \emph{pre}$\to$\emph{op} chain. Third, across all 24
cells the \emph{pre}$\to$\emph{op} chain spans
\textbf{632$\times$--51,430$\times$} and \emph{pre}$\to$\emph{routed}
spans 329$\times$--31,905$\times$. The table
keeps its own floor honestly: at $k{=}2048$, $N{=}2{,}048$ op and proxy
tie (15.74 vs.\ 15.73\us{}) --- a 2,048-wide row admits no column
parallelism.

The routed column rides a TILE-native I/O landing: TILE input by
layout dispatch, opt-in TILE output and uint16 indices, deleting the
tilize/untilize envelope where $k$ rounds to
${\leq}1024$ ($-67$ to $-84\%$); at $k{=}2048$ the tilize chain
measured cheaper and is kept --- per-shape policy by measurement
(\S\ref{sec:eval-attribution}).

The closest internal comparison is a fused attention program embedding
a top-k reduction: at its native cell ($k{=}2048$,
$N{=}65{,}536$) it measures 24.4\us{} on 129 cores, $1.4\times$ below
the op's 34.3\us{}. Both caveats matter: that time \emph{includes} the
attention work fused around the selection, and it is a one-call-site
fused program, not a callable operator; a per-core breakdown was not
committed, so we claim no more than the two end-to-end numbers.

\subsection{Strong Scaling Versus the Cost Model}
\label{sec:eval-scaling}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pscaling} sweeps the slice count $P$ on two cells, 15
measured points total (the factory refuses $P{=}1$ --- single-core
service is the
row-parallel path's job). At $k{=}2048$, $N{=}65{,}536$ the op falls
$183.0 \to 34.4$\us{} from $P{=}2$ to $P{=}32$; at $k{=}512$,
$N{=}262{,}144$ it falls $560.2 \to 23.3$\us{} from $P{=}2$ to
$P{=}104$. Both curves are monotone in $P$ (\S\ref{sec:system}), and
the $k{=}512$ curve flattens as $P$
approaches the 130-core grid. L1 never bounds $P$: per-slice fetches
are 2--8\,KB and the read stream sits 10--100$\times$ below op time.

The overlay is \S\ref{sec:system}'s cost model times its two measured
merge-unit constants --- no further fitting. It tracks the $k{=}2048$
curve within 4.3\% at every point and predicts the non-monotone bump at
$P{=}24$: $\lceil 32/24 \rceil = 2$ leaf chunks plus a fifth tree
level. On
$k{=}512$ it lands within 0.3\% at the chosen $P{=}104$ and
overestimates only the deep-serial tail the factory never picks
($+33\%$ at $P{=}2$, Fig.~\ref{fig:pscaling}) --- the model is
accurate where it
decides.

\subsection{Chunk-Skip A/B}
\label{sec:eval-skip}

The row-parallel chunk-skip of \S\ref{sec:system} was A/B-measured on
five cells, three arms each (baseline, ungated, gated). On the shapes the
small-k routing ships here, the gated skip wins
$\mathbf{279.14 \to 153.19}$\us{} ($-45.1\%$, $1.82\times$) at
rows${=}2$, $k{=}32$, $N{=}65{,}536$, and $\to 177.94$\us{}
($-36.3\%$) at rows${=}8$ --- the rows${=}8$ win is smaller because the
op time is the slowest of eight i.i.d.\ rows. On the three cells where
the skip law says no win exists, the gate holds the line: $+0.04\%$ to
$+0.51\%$, the largest being gate-off code growth, not the skip test. The column-parallel guard cell
re-measures at 13.15\us{} with byte-identical kernels.

These measurements close the forecast's loop (\S\ref{sec:forecast}).
The law itself is now confirmed on-device: compile-time-gated skip
telemetry --- its disabled path proven byte-identical by
ELF-disassembly diff --- recorded 400 i.i.d.\ rows at
$k{=}32$, $N{=}65{,}536$ (128 chunks); per-position skip rates track
$e^{-K/(c+1)}$ at all 120 tested positions (the dots of
Fig.~\ref{fig:skiplaw}), and rows average 65.11 skips against the
law's 66.62 ($-2.3\%$). A
gate-constant ablation closes the $K/4$ choice: divisors
2/4/8 move the $k{=}32$ cells by at most $-0.8\%$ while $/8$ regresses
$k{=}512$ by $+3.2\%$ --- ties where Eq.~\ref{eq:skiplaw}
predicts them --- so $K/4$ stands.

\subsection{Production Call-Site Scenarios}
\label{sec:eval-scenarios}

Table~\ref{tab:scenarios} replays eight production call-site shapes on
the same gated harness --- the data contract's three consumer classes
(\S\ref{sec:bg-problem}). The wins concentrate
where production is single-core today. The Qwen3 shape that opened the
paper improves from its 9,586.9\us{}
single-core chain to 213.6\us{} routed and 193.6\us{} direct
($44.9$--$49.5\times$); split-vocab sampling (deliberately
unpadded, $N{=}64{,}128$) behaves the same:
$8{,}924.0 \to 210.9$\us{} routed, 188.0\us{} direct.

The TP${=}8$
shape already rides the multi-core bitonic:
routed measures $1.00\times$ --- the honest already-fast row (the
direct op still gains $1.4\times$). The sparse-attention indexer rows
already call the op by name --- today \emph{is} the op --- and forcing
them through generic routing
would cost 22--26\%. The MoE gate rows were shapes the routing
predicate declined at the study's start (the $W \geq 4{,}096$ floor);
a measured eligibility carve-out ($k \leq 16$, padded $W \leq 512$,
$\leq$32 rows) now captures them automatically: $24.2 \to 8.18$\us{}
and $77.4 \to 8.34$\us{} ($2.96\times$/$9.29\times$) with zero
model-code changes.

One caveat is mandatory: the production sampling call sites pass
pre-allocated index tensors, sub-core grid pinning, and
\texttt{stable=True} --- each independently disqualifies routing as-is.
The routed column is the \emph{canonical} call form with
those arguments dropped --- a one-line call-site change whose
bit-exactness \S\ref{sec:eval-attribution} audits, not a free win ---
and the end-to-end tokens/s effect of that change is unmeasured.

\subsection{Attribution: Where the Speedup Comes From}
\label{sec:eval-attribution}

Assembled, the exhibits answer the question a four-orders-of-magnitude
claim owes its reader: what got faster? \emph{Mostly the routing.} The
anchor and the production sampling shapes all fail the incumbent's
eligibility gates (\S\ref{sec:bg-incumbent}) and ran the single-core
catch-all while 129 cores idled. Along Table~\ref{tab:competition}'s
own arms, the anchor's $18{,}401\times$ \emph{pre}$\to$\emph{op} chain
factors exactly: $1.17\times$ from micro-op wins inside the stock
kernels (\S\ref{sec:char-floors}); $1{,}511\times$ available on a
\emph{single} core --- the proxy arm
measures 357.0\us{} against stock's 539.5\,ms, an algorithm
the incumbent shipped but declined to route this shape to;
$10.4\times$ from the tree on 32 cores. Escape from the fallback, not a
magic kernel, carries the orders of magnitude; the honest
apples-to-apples, Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}'s TP${=}8$ row where the
stock multi-core engine \emph{is} eligible, reads
$171.2 \to 120.8$\us{}, $1.4\times$. Core-for-core the honesty holds
at small $k$ too: one row-parallel core selects top-32 of 65,536 in
${\approx}194$\us{} wall against the stock factory's ${\approx}300$\us{}
per row ($9{,}586.9/32$, Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}) --- the $49.5\times$
there is $32\times$ parallelism $\times$ ${\approx}1.5\times$ per-core
algorithm, a factor that grows to the $1{,}511\times$ above as the
factory's cost explodes with $k$.

That $1.4\times$ --- and the parallel term, $10.4\times$ at the anchor,
growing to $43\times$ at $k{=}512$, $N{=}262{,}144$ --- is the
algorithmic content, each term
separately measured: the log-tree of Algorithm~\ref{alg:operator} over
the serial $P{-}1$ chain,
1.66--2.01$\times$ at fixed core counts plus monotonicity in $P$
(\S\ref{sec:tree}); the cost model with its rectangle embedding,
8--27\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:pscaling}, \S\ref{sec:costmodel}); the law-gated
chunk skip, $-45.1\%$/$-36.3\%$ on the long streams it opens for and,
by Eq.~\ref{eq:skiplaw}, provably ${\approx}0$ on the 1--5-chunk
streams the column split produces --- hence the gate
(Fig.~\ref{fig:skiplaw}, \S\ref{sec:skip-law}).
The parallel term carries its applicability boundary: the tree
converts cores into \emph{latency} at ${\approx}1.0$ core-efficiency
--- $P{=}2$ costs 183.0\us{} (\S\ref{sec:eval-scaling}), 366
core-\textmu s per row, against the shipped per-row kernel's 357.0\us{}
on one core ($+2.5\%$) --- so the $10.4\times$ serves latency-bound
low-row callers (the decode-shape indexer, low-batch tails), while
callers with rows ${\geq}$ grid are already core-optimal on the
one-core-per-row layout: the 160-row prefill indexer of
Table~\ref{tab:scenarios} measures 712.3\us{}, almost exactly two
130-core row-waves of that kernel ($2 \times 357.0$), reported there
as $1.00\times$/$0.80\times$. Cutting per-core work at
that shape is the fusion lever below, not more cores.
One term is structural: selection, never sorting --- only
$M$-wide candidate windows cross the NoC, $P{-}1$ in total
(Fig.~\ref{fig:operator}, \S\ref{sec:tree}). Why these moves and not
GPU-style counting is \S\ref{sec:design-space}'s verdict: the machine
sells oblivious comparison cheaply and decisions
affordably but materialization dearly, so a parallelized comparison
network --- work skipped, never data compacted --- is the
architecturally consistent primitive.

What the design does \emph{not} win on is equally measured. The last
two data columns of Table~\ref{tab:competition} hold the op against the
vendor's published roofline, with its $k$-dependent multipliers
(0.612/0.850/1.000)~\cite{ttllmperf2026}:
$9.7\times$--$29.9\times$ across all 24 cells, shrinking monotonically
with $N$ at every $k$. Its contributors are measured elsewhere
